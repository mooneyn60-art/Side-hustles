# Fact pack: why do cats knead? (slug cats-knead)

Research date: 2026-11-15. Searched phrase: "why do cats knead" (Google "why do cats" interest: knead 44, and "do biscuits" 40, in research/95, 2026-11-12; not re-measured here). "Why does my cat knead me" is the same question in the first person.

How each page was opened: [fetch] = I opened the live page on 2026-11-15 and copied the sentence from the returned page text. Date checked for every row: 2026-11-15.

## 0. Read this first
- MAIN FACT: CONFIRMED from International Cat Care (updated 26 September 2025). "Kittens suckle their mothers by treading with alternate front paws on her abdomen to stimulate the flow of milk." If a cat kneads on your lap or chest, "this shows that they associate your relationship with that of their mother when they were a kitten." [fetch]
- SECOND FACT: CONFIRMED from Emma Grigg, animal behaviorist with the UC Davis School of Veterinary Medicine (UC Davis Magazine, April 19, 2021). Kneading "is most likely carried over from their days as kittens." On a favorite person or bed, "it's a self-soothing behavior." She adds, "With humans, it's almost like a sign of the bond." [fetch]
- Independent pages: International Cat Care also says some cats drool as they "anticipate the milk-feed that kneading usually predicted," that a few cats retain the behavior into adulthood when they feel safe or on a fluffy cushion or blanket, that kneading is a perfectly normal behavior, and that cats should never be punished for it. All [fetch], 2026-11-15.
- Hedge the pet science. iCatCare's "this shows" and Grigg's "most likely" / "almost like a sign of the bond" are behavioral readings, not a lab result about every cat. Do not say "your cat thinks you are its mother" as a proven fact, or that kneading always means the cat is happy.
- Not found on these opened pages: a required number of kneads, a claim that kneading always marks territory with paw scent glands (a popular extra; not quoted here because it was not on the iCatCare or UC Davis kneading pages opened today), or a medical diagnosis attached to ordinary kneading.

## 2. Three hook facts
1. CK-F1 + CK-F6: Kneading most likely starts as a kitten nursing action — treading to help milk let down.
2. CK-F2 + CK-F7: On a lap, a person, or a bed, the opened pages read it as a carry-over / self-soothing behavior, and almost like a sign of the bond. Hedge it.
3. CK-F5: It is a normal behavior. iCatCare says not to punish the cat for it.

## 4. Shortened-claim traps
- "It's a kitten thing" without "treading to stimulate milk" or "most likely."
- "It means they love you" instead of "almost like a sign of the bond."
- Voicing Grigg's “You are someone who is important to me” as the cat's words.

## 5. Disagreements between sources
iCatCare is more definite ("this shows"). Grigg is more hedged ("most likely," "almost like"). They agree on the kitten nursing origin. A short line can use "most likely" and still credit both.

## 6. Could not open
- No official AVMA or ASPCA page on kneading was found and opened on this pass.
- A Cornell alumni interview that mentions kneading as a sign of affection was not used as a primary source (alumni magazine, not the Feline Health Center).
