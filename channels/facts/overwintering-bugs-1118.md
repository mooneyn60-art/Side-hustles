# Fact pack: bugs that come indoors when it gets cold (slug overwintering-bugs-1118)

Research date: 2026-11-18. Status: SOURCED (20 facts) for brown marmorated stink bug (BMSB), multicolored Asian lady beetle, and boxelder bug. All Extension or university.
How each page was checked: opened live on 2026-11-18 through the page reader (quotes of 125 characters or less, some shortened; the checker should reopen the page). Not used: Washington State (HTTP 403), Virginia Tech BMSB page (404 after redirect).

## 0. Read this first
- MAIN FACT: all three bugs look for a sheltered place for winter and your house is one (F1, F9, F14).
- CALM-DOWN FACT: the BMSB and boxelder bug do not harm people or houses (F3, F4, F13, F15).
- CONFLICT to keep honest: Penn State says lady beetles "occasionally bite people" (F10). Illinois Extension says "they can't break skin and only cause minor discomfort" (F11). Say "can nip" with the Penn State name, or leave bite out.
- Hedge it: "especially", "preferred", "rarely".
- Not found: indoor reproduction for lady beetles (Penn State page silent); BMSB first US detection year (not on the pages opened). Do not state either.

## 2. Three hook facts
1. F1 + F5: the stink bug is not breaking in to hurt you; it wants winter shelter. Sealing the house is the best fix.
2. F12 + F10/F11: the lady beetle leaks a stinky yellow fluid. Whether it bites depends on which Extension you ask.
3. F17 + F20: boxelder bugs arrive in August and September, and they like female boxelder trees.

## 3. Traps
- Do not say all three "come in the same way". Sources are per species.
- Do not say lady beetles are "bad ladybugs". Illinois says "They can outcompete native lady beetles and other predatory insects for food." (same Illinois page as F11). That is a claim about food, not about homes: keep it out of a house-bug video.
- No pesticide advice (F7, F15).
- Two pages are old (Clemson 2014, Wisconsin 2010).
