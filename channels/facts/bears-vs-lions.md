## 0. Read this first
- MAIN FACT: CONFIRMED from a primary Park Service page today. Katmai National Park & Preserve (last updated October 29, 2024, printed on the page) says "Most adult males typically weigh 600-900 pounds (272-408 kg) in mid-summer. - SECOND FACT: CONFIRMED from the Smithsonian's National Zoo today. A male African lion (*Panthera leo*) "weigh[s] from 330 to 550 pounds (150 to 250 kilograms)." [raw]
- Independent pages opened: San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance lion fact sheet; San Diego Zoo public lion page; U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service grizzly species page; Yellowstone Bear Ecology (last updated April 18, 2025); Yellowstone grizzly facts page (last updated December 21, 2015); Yellowstone "Characteristics of Bears" page (last updated October 23, 2017); Glacier Bears (last updated November 6, 2017); NPS Staying Safe Around Bears (last updated September 8, 2025); Yellowstone Safety (for the 40-feet-per-second bear-speed line). Do not say a generic "grizzly would probably win."

## 4. Shortened-claim traps
- "Katmai bears weigh 1,000 pounds" drops mid-summer vs fall, "typically," "most adult males," and "large adult males can weigh well over."
- "Grizzlies average 400 to 600 pounds" drops "in the 48 contiguous states" and "adult males" (USFWS).
- "Yellowstone males weigh 200 to 700 pounds" is the ecology page; other Yellowstone pages give averages or a slightly different range.
- "Lions weigh 330 to 550 pounds" drops "male," "African," and the Smithsonian credit, and disagrees with other zoo pages.
- "Would probably win" without "on size" and "nobody has tested it" becomes an invented winner.
- "Brown bear" and "grizzly" used as if they were one weight class (BL-F15).
