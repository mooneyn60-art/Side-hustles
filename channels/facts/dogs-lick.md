# Fact pack: why do dogs lick you? (slug dogs-lick)

Research date: 2026-11-15. Searched phrase: "why do dogs lick you" (Google web interest 100 on that seed in research/95, 2026-11-12; not re-measured here). "Why does my dog lick me" is the same question in the first person.

How each page was opened: [fetch] = I opened the live page on 2026-11-15 and copied the sentence from the returned page text. [raw] = I fetched the file (University of Pennsylvania School of Veterinary Medicine PDF) on 2026-11-15 and copied the sentence from the extracted text. Date checked for every row: 2026-11-15.

## 0. Read this first
- MAIN FACT: CONFIRMED from the University of Pennsylvania School of Veterinary Medicine (Ryan Veterinary Hospital, "Dog Body Language Basics"). "Dog licks person or other dog: not a “kiss”. This is a greeting behavior, but if dog licks someone repeatedly, he may be anxious about that dog or person." [raw]
- SECOND FACT: CONFIRMED from the same handout, as a different signal: "Dog licks his lips: anxiety about a situation or interaction." And later: "Dogs do not kiss either, and they may be frightened by kissing." Lip licking is not the same behavior as licking a person. [raw]
- Independent pages opened: Cummings School of Veterinary Medicine at Tufts (board-certified behaviorist Stephanie Borns-Weil): anxious dogs "may also appear extra friendly and continually approach, jump on someone, and lick their face." AAHA 2015 behavior-guidelines table: "Lip licking" and "Nose licking" are among common signs of nonspecific anxiety or distress in dogs and cats. Texas A&M College of Veterinary Medicine: "Licking lips — often accompanied with yawning" is listed among signs of a nervous or alerted dog. All [fetch], 2026-11-15.
- Hedge the pet science. These pages use "may," list more than one reading, and separate greeting licks from lip licks. Do not say licking you "is a kiss," "always means love," or "always means anxiety." Context matters (a single greeting lick vs repeated licking; lip licking vs licking a person).
- Not found on these pages: a claim that dogs lick people for salt, for food taste, or to "show they love you" as a proven result. Those popular lines stay out unless a vet or agency page opened here says them.

## 2. Three hook facts
1. DL-F1: Penn Vet says licking a person is not a "kiss." It is a greeting behavior, but repeated licking may mean the dog is anxious about that person.
2. DL-F2 + DL-F6 + DL-F7: Lip licking is a different signal, listed by Penn Vet, AAHA, and Texas A&M among anxiety or nervous signs. Do not confuse it with licking you.
3. DL-F4: Tufts says an anxious dog may also appear extra friendly and lick a face. "May," not "always."

## 4. Shortened-claim traps
- "It's not a kiss" without "greeting" and without "repeated licking may be anxiety."
- "Licking means anxiety" without "repeated," "may," or the lip-vs-face distinction.
- Reciting AAHA's lip-licking list as if it explained why dogs lick your face.

## 5. Disagreements between sources
No source opened here contradicts Penn Vet's "not a kiss / greeting / repeated licking may be anxiety." They add other, related signals (lip licking; a face lick that may come with an extra-friendly anxious presentation). The disagreement is with the popular "it's a kiss / it means love" reading, not among these pages.
