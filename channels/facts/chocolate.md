# Fact pack: can dogs eat chocolate? (slug chocolate)

Research date: 2026-11-15. Searched phrase: "can dogs eat chocolate" (YouTube and Google rank 1 in research/95, 2026-11-12; not re-measured here). Timed for the December holiday window (packaging slot Tue 12/01/2026 16:00 ET). The pack also records what the same sources say about cats, so a dog-only line is not flattened onto cats.

## 0. Read this first
- MAIN FACT: CONFIRMED from the FDA ("Leave Chocolate Out of Rover's Celebrations"). "Chocolate is toxic to dogs." "Chocolate contains more theobromine than caffeine; it’s the theobromine that’s the main culprit in chocolate toxicity in dogs." "Theobromine can cause mild signs in dogs with doses as low as 9 mg per pound of body weight, and severe signs starting around 18 mg per pound of body weight." [fetch]
- SECOND FACT: CONFIRMED from the same FDA page's table (approximate theobromine in 1 ounce): milk chocolate 57 mg; semisweet chocolate chips 136 mg; dark chocolate with 70-85% cacao 227 mg; unsweetened baking chocolate 364 mg. "The darker the chocolate, the higher the cocoa content, and therefore, the more theobromine the chocolate contains." [fetch]
- Independent pages opened: ASPCA "What to Do If Your Pet Gets into Chocolate" and "People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets"; Merck Veterinary Manual, "Chocolate Toxicosis in Animals" (full review Sept 2024, last updated Feb 2026); UC Davis School of Veterinary Medicine, "Chocolate Poisoning in Dogs"; AVMA household-hazards brochure (2022), which lists chocolate among kitchen foods that can be harmful or deadly. All opened 2026-11-15.
- Do not invent a dose threshold. Every number in a script must be quoted with its unit and its source. The FDA speaks in mg per pound of body weight and mg per ounce of chocolate. Merck speaks in mg/kg (and gives a milk-chocolate figure of about 1 oz/lb as potentially lethal). UC Davis speaks in ounces of chocolate per pound of body weight. Do not convert one system into another and present the result as the source's number.
- Do not say any amount is safe, do not give one dose for all dogs, and do not say a bite always kills. Size, type of chocolate, and individual sensitivity all matter (FDA).
- Call for help as the sources word it. FDA: "If your pet is sick or has a veterinary emergency, call your veterinarian. If your veterinarian is unavailable, call your local emergency animal hospital or an animal poison control center." ASPCA: "please contact your veterinarian or call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435."
- Cats: the ASPCA says cats are sensitive to theobromine just like dogs; they tend not to be drawn to chocolate and are less likely to eat large amounts, but if a cat eats chocolate, follow the same advice. Do not say chocolate is harmless to cats.

## 2. Three hook facts
1. CH-F1 + CH-F4: Chocolate is toxic to dogs. The FDA says theobromine can cause mild signs at doses as low as 9 mg per pound of body weight, and severe signs around 18.
2. CH-F3: Dark vs milk, as the FDA states it: about 227 mg theobromine per ounce in 70-85% dark chocolate, about 57 mg in milk chocolate.
3. CH-F6 / CH-F10: If the pet is sick, or if you think it ate chocolate, call your veterinarian or a poison control line, in the source's words.

## 4. Shortened-claim traps
- "9 mg per pound" without "theobromine," "as low as," "mild signs," "of body weight," and "the FDA says."
- "Dark is worse" without a figure, or with a figure that is not the page's.
- "Call poison control" without "your veterinarian" if the page leads with the vet.
- Using the FDA Lab-vs-Chihuahua story as if every 6-ounce bag is a death sentence for every small dog; the page is an example, with arithmetic that is already on the page.
