## The "no tactic saved Meatball" rule, and the ruling

**Exact text**, `/workspace/skills-readonly/skills/channel-brain/MASTER_ORDERS.md` (and the same file under `/workspace/channel-context/skills/channel-brain/`), line 137, rules the 11/12 check added for pet and survival scripts, item (7):

> (7) No ending says a tactic saved Meatball: "Meatball lived. Nothing's guaranteed."

`index.md` quotes the same sentence back to that line. The Meatball bible ( `/workspace/drafts/meatball-bible.md` and `/workspace/notebook/channels/meatball-bible.md`) treats that closer as the survival-episode ending and records the reason it exists:

> "Meatball lived. Nothing's guaranteed." The survival-episode closer, then the loop question. … A KNOWLEDGE note (11/12, research 92) dropped "ran like hell, and lived" where it could be heard as the tactic saving him. Keep the hedge.

**Ruling.** Rule 7 is not a ban on every condition in a verdict. It bans an ending that says a *tactic* saved Meatball. "Meatball lived. Nothing's guaranteed." is the prescribed closer when the alternative would credit a move. A condition may stay only if it *scopes* a sourced fact (where the animal lives; an official rule already given as a safety instruction) and cannot be heard as a how-to-survive tactic someone would imitate. If the condition attaches "probably [survives]" to a place or a move a viewer could copy, it breaks the rule. The fallback for each, if the condition fails that test, is **"Meatball lived. Nothing's guaranteed."**

| Verdict | Ruling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lion: "probably, from inside the car" | **Breaks rule 7.** Required fix: use the fallback. | "From inside the car" is a move. Pairing it with "probably" is the same shape as "ran like hell, and lived": the tactic is what saved him. Kruger's rule is real and is already said clean as the safety line ("stay in your vehicle unless in a designated area," with windows and doors on the card). Keep that as the instruction. Do not make it the verdict. |
| Hippo: "out of its territory, probably" | **Breaks rule 7, and overreaches the source.** Required fix: use the fallback, or a warning-shaped line that does not grant a safe zone. | Live San Diego Zoo: "An unsuspecting human boater who enters hippo territory risks an aggressive response." That is a risk if you enter, not a probably-safe-if-you-stay-out guarantee. The same page says female hippos, especially mothers with calves, can be equally dangerous, and that hippos can invade human areas such as farmlands. "Out of its territory, probably" invents a boundary a viewer cannot judge and might copy. |
| Komodo: "probably, unless he's on a few Indonesian islands" | **Borderline. Recommended rephrase, not a required cut.** | The condition is the Smithsonian range fact ("limited to a few Indonesian islands of the Lesser Sunda group"), not a visitor move. The map correctly lights Komodo, Rintja and Flores, and leaves Padar off (not seen there since the 1970s). It can still be heard as "avoid those islands and you're fine." Prefer a range fact plus the fallback, for example: "They're limited to a few Indonesian islands. Meatball lived. Nothing's guaranteed." |

The saltwater-crocodile verdict, "in the water, probably not," is the other direction — a sourced warning (Australian Museum CB50), not a how-to — and can stay. The alligator verdict, "probably," rests on "serious injuries are rare in Florida," with "Nothing's guaranteed" and the FWC safety line just before it; that is rarity, not a tactic, and must not be heard as "alligators are safe." The polar-bear verdict, "a swim race? Probably not," is a comparison to one record swim, not a survival tactic.
