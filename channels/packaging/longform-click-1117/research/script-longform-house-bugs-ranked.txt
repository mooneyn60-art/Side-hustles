Number one on this list is probably in your basement right now. Every bug in your house, ranked. Harmless, all the way to nightmare. And three of them land where you'd never guess. Guess along with me. We start at the bottom. You flip on the basement light. Something jumps at your face. The camel cricket. Humped back, giant legs, crazy long antennae. No wings. Can't chirp. Jumps anyway, just to scare you. In one study, nine in ten houses with them had an Asian species. It eats dead bits and rotting plants. Your basement's cleanup crew. The Asian one first showed up here in 1898, in a Minnesota greenhouse. My roommate screamed louder than the cricket. And crickets can't even chirp. Where does it go? Lock it in. Three. Two. One. Harmless. Totally harmless to you. Next: a spider with eight eyes on you. The fuzzy spider on your windowsill is watching you. Right now. No web. It stalks, then pounces. Up to forty times its body length. At night it hangs from a silk thread. Scientists caught its eyes twitching. A sleep-like state. It might be dreaming about you. Probably not. Its bite is usually not serious. It would rather eat your flies. It hunts in the daytime, on your plants and across your floor. Your guess? Lock it in. Three. Two. One. Harmless. And the next one? It looks like pure nightmare fuel. Up to thirty legs, sprinting across your bathroom floor. The house centipede. Everyone screams. Everyone squishes. Here's why that's a mistake. It hunts roaches, bed bugs, silverfish and carpet beetle larvae. Grab a leg, and the leg pops right off. It's built to break. Can it sting you? Only if handled roughly. Experts say that's rare. It's free pest control, working nights. That fly on your banana? It probably rode home on it. One female can lay about five hundred eggs. Egg to adult? About a week. Your fruit bowl is a nursery. Your book club has a silent member. It's eating the glue. Your call. Lock it in. Three. Two. One. Damaging. Dry the air, box up old paper. Next: the cutest bug here. Tiny, round, polka dots.
