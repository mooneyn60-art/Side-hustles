## Sourced rules

### YouTube

1. **Hashtag hard limit is 60, not 15.** YouTube Help, [Find playlists & videos using hashtags](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658): "If a video or playlist has more than 60 hashtags, we'll ignore each hashtag on that content." Over-tagging may also remove the video from uploads or search. Many older guides still say 15; that figure is from an earlier version of the same Help page. Our house cap stays at 3 to 5 relevant hashtags, which is well under either number.

2. **Up to three description hashtags appear by the title, and YouTube picks the "most engaging" ones.** Same Help page: "Out of all the hashtags you add to the video’s description, up to three hashtags that are considered most engaging will appear by your video title." Older guides said "the first three." Official text now says YouTube chooses by engagement, not by order. All hashtags still stay in the description and still link to a hashtag results page.

3. **Hashtags must be directly related, with no spaces, no profanity, and no keyword stuffing.** Same Help page: no spaces (`#TwoWords`); "Don't add hashtags that are not directly related to the video or playlist. Misleading or unrelated hashtags may result in the removal of your video or playlist"; "Use of profanity or offensive terms in your hashtags may lead to an age restriction or removal"; "adding ordinary descriptive tags or repetitive sentences in the description is (still) prohibited."

4. **Titles should be accurate and short, with the important words first.** YouTube Help, [Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300): be accurate; "aim to keep it short and put the most important words near the beginning. Save episode numbers and branding for the end"; "Limit ALL CAPS and emoji." YouTube Help, [Get started creating YouTube Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070): Shorts titles max out at 100 characters.

5. **The first lines of the description should say what the video is about and use 1–2 main keywords that also appear in the title.** YouTube Help, [Tips for video descriptions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449): "Consider using the first few lines of your description to describe your video because it’s what viewers will see first." Each video needs a unique description. "Identify 1-2 main words that describe your video and feature them prominently in both your description and title." Max 5,000 characters.

6. **`#Shorts` is allowed when the video is a Short, but it is not required.** The hashtag Help page does not say `#Shorts` is mandatory. We still add it last on YouTube to match the existing Meatball Shorts pattern (3 topic tags + `#meatballmyths` + `#shorts`).

### TikTok

7. **Captions should add context; hashtags should describe the video; more hashtags do not guarantee more reach.** TikTok Newsroom, [5 tips for TikTok creators](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/5-tips-for-tiktok-creators): "Use the space in captions to add context and share more about a video." "Add hashtags to your captions that are relevant to your videos so they're more likely to be found by the right audiences. More hashtags won't guarantee a broader reach, and there's only so much space in your captions, so only add hashtags that best represent what your video is about."

8. **`#FYP`, `#ForYou`, and `#ForYouPage` do not improve For You chances.** Same Newsroom post: those tags "work just like all other hashtags on TikTok, so adding these to your caption won’t necessarily improve your chances of getting on someone’s For You feed." "Instead, we recommend using the space in your caption to add context to your video along with hashtags that are relevant to your content." Do not use `#fyp` or `#foryou` here.

9. **TikTok Search uses the caption, on-screen text, and hashtags to judge relevance. A long list of hashtags is a myth.** TikTok Creator Academy, [Getting discovered with Search](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/Getting-discovered-with-Search), as quoted in current secondary writeups of that page (the Academy page itself is a JavaScript app and did not return extractable text to a plain fetch this turn): "Videos in search results are sorted according to a combination of factors, including relevance to your search query and engagement. Relevance is determined based on things like video captions, video text, and hashtags." The same article calls "Using a long list of hashtags will help your video rank higher in Search" a myth, and says "Overloading your caption with loosely related keywords or hashtags can actually work against you. What matters most is using relevant, accurate language that clearly reflects what your video is about." It also warns that stuffing unrelated keywords into on-screen text "can get your video flagged for spam." Use the searched phrase in the first caption line, in plain sentences, not as a keyword dump.

10. **Caption length: official TikTok sources say 2,200 characters, not 4,000.** TikTok Newsroom, [Inspiring creativity with our new editing tools](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/editing-tools) (Oct 6, 2022): "You can now pair your videos and content made in Photo Mode with longer descriptions, up to 2,200 characters for each post." TikTok for Developers, [Content Posting API](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-posting-api-reference-direct-post), caps the caption at "2200 in UTF-16 runes." That is the limit scheduling tools use. The 4,000-character figure appears in some later third-party writeups, sometimes about photo posts; I could not open a current official TikTok page that states 4,000 for video captions. Keep every caption well under 2,200 so Metricool and the API will accept it.
