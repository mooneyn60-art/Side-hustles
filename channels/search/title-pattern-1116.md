# Meatball Myths: one title pattern for pet-POV episodes (search evidence)

Checked **Mon Nov 16, 2026, ~6:45–7:30 PM ET** by the Search agent. Read-only: nothing posted, scheduled, or changed. Builds on `channels/research/deep-growth-research-1116.md` §2 "Patterns" item 3 and §5 #5 ("Own one title pattern").

**Labels:** `SEEN` = pulled myself today (YouTube autocomplete, TikTok suggestion API, yt-dlp search). `3P` = third-party Google *web* search volume (not YouTube, not TikTok). `ANALYSIS` = my inference. No search volume or view count here comes from anywhere other than a listed source.

**Rulings (Nov 16, 2026, Social Studio):**
1. **"?" in titles is approved** for pet-POV episodes. It overrides `title-formula.md` rule 1.
2. **Meatball is the human stick figure, not a pet.** Dog episodes are "Inside Noodle's …" and cat episodes are "Inside Sauce's …". This is settled.
3. **TikTok follows MASTER_ORDERS:** 5 topic hashtags and no brand tag.
4. **Toxic-food episodes** (chocolate, grape, chicken bone, gum, and any later ones) must end on the sourced ASPCA Animal Poison Control line, with its condition kept (see §3).
5. **Cats kneading (#9)** is a re-title of the existing `cats-knead` episode, not a new episode.

## 1. The pattern

> **`[Exact searched question]? Inside [Pet]'s [Body Part]`**
>
> e.g. **Why Do Dogs Eat Grass? Inside Noodle's Stomach**

- **Front half = the exact phrase people type** (YouTube autocomplete or TikTok suggestion wording), so it stays in the first 40 characters.
- **Back half = the pet-POV promise.** We go inside the pet's body, where the organ characters talk. One body part per episode.
- **[Pet]:** Noodle for dog questions, Sauce for cat questions. Settled: Meatball is the human stick figure. He can appear in the story, but the POV body is always the pet's.
- **No series tags, no "Ep N", no "| Meatball Myths".** Target length ≤ 60 characters. Shorts use the same title (the question alone is acceptable if a Short runs long).

### Why this one (evidence, all SEEN unless labeled)

1. **People search the questions, not the organ format.**
   - YouTube autocomplete for `why do dogs` returns lick you, eat grass, howl, lick their paws, eat poop, tilt their head, bark, wag their tails, as its top 8.
   - `what happens if a dog eats` returns protein bar, chocolate, grape, chicken bone, gum.
   - TikTok's suggestion API returns the same families (`why do dogs eat grass` #2 under `why do dogs eat`; `what happens if a dog eats a grape?` #1 and `…chocolate` #2 under `what happens if a dog eats`).
   - `your dog's organs when` and `your cat's organs` return **no organ completions** on YouTube. Only "your dog just died" and "your dog if you" came back.
   - So the Zoo Inside's pet-organ format gets its views from browse and suggested, not search. The question has to lead.
2. **Exact-question titles hold the top search slots for these queries.** Examples from the yt-dlp `ytsearch20` results:
   - SciShow, "Why Do Dogs Eat Grass?": 750,503
   - SciShow, "Why Do Cats Purr?": 1,895,656
   - SciShow, "Why Do Cats Knead?": 1,400,436
   - BrainStuff, "Why Do Dogs Tilt Their Heads?": 1,436,465
   - Colossal Cranium, "Why Do Cats Hate Water? | COLOSSAL QUESTIONS": 3,611,280
   - Colossal Cranium's titles are the same shape as ours: question first, then a branded tail.
3. **The "inside the body" POV is proven, at small channel sizes.**
   - The Zoo Inside, "Your Dog's Organs When She Holds Her Pee All Day": **73,736** (yt-dlp search, today). It was 72,337 in deep-growth earlier today.
   - Organ Crew (2.1K subscribers per yt-dlp) has "Your Organs When You Wake Up During Surgery" at 288K and "Your Organs the Morning After a Party" at 125K (rounded channel-tab counts).
   - On YouTube, `your organs when` autocompletes 9 human versions (fasting, 10,000 steps, sugar, creatine…), so the format is understood. Nobody owns the pet version of it in search.
4. **Third-party Google volume (`3P`, web search, not YouTube):**
   - Glimpse (last updated Feb 2, 2026), past 30 days: "why do dogs eat grass" 14,059; "why do dogs lick you" 13,197; "why do cats purr" 10,744.
   - Fox Magazine / WhatAnimalsEat (Ahrefs, global monthly): "why do dogs eat grass" 82,000; "why do dogs lick you" 60,000; "why do dogs eat poop" 45,000; "what colors can dogs see" 34,000.
5. **Our own data** (from `growth-research-1116.md`, cited in deep-growth §5 #5): the question title got 2,390 views vs 1–2 for the answer-title remake of the same topic.

**Rejected:**
- "Your Dog's Organs When…": no autocomplete, and one 6-day-old channel owns it.
- "Why It Sucks To Be Born As…": deep-growth calls it crowded; Bound's newest uploads are at 1.4K–6.4K.
- "If Your Dog Ever… DON'T…": horror tone.
- The organ-first "[Name]'s [Organ] When…" idea (deep-growth §5 #5): no searched words in the first 40 characters.

**Decided against `channels/packaging/title-formula.md`** (Social Studio, Nov 16):
- **Rule 1** (no "?" in the core title): **overridden for pet-POV episodes.** The "?" is approved because the question is the search match.
- **Rule 6** (series tag last): overridden by the no-series-tag order.
- **Rule 5** (one emoji): not ruled on. It stays optional; if used, it goes after the body part.

## 3. Templates

### YouTube title
`{Exact Question in Title Case}? Inside {Noodle|Sauce}'s {Body Part}` (Noodle = dog, Sauce = cat). ≤ 60 characters, no series tag.

### YouTube description
```
{exact searched question}? {One-line hook: who, the clock, the stake. e.g. "Noodle ate grass 10 minutes ago. The stomach just noticed."}

{1–2 sentences answering the question with the checked fact and its source named in plain words (e.g. "The FDA says…"). Keep the hedge.}

Chapters
00:00 {exact searched question}?
{mm:ss} Inside {Pet}'s {Body Part}
{mm:ss} {Second searched sub-question, e.g. "Is it bad if my dog eats grass?"}
{mm:ss} {Third sub-question / myth check}
{mm:ss} What vets say (when to call your vet)

Sources
- {Agency / paper}: {URL}
- {Agency / paper}: {URL}

More from inside {Pet}: {playlist link}
Not veterinary advice. {Toxic-food episodes: the ASPCA close below, word for word, with its URL.}

#{questionnospaces} #{species}facts #{bodypart or topic} #meatballmyths #shorts(only on Shorts)
```
- **Chapter titles = more searched phrasings.** Take them from the autocomplete lists in this file, e.g. "why do dogs eat grass and throw up" (YT and TT both suggest it).
- **Hashtags (YouTube only):** 3 topic + the channel brand tag (+ `#shorts` on Shorts), per the written 11/12 YouTube rule in `title-formula.md`. Social Studio's ruling covered TikTok only, so YouTube is unchanged.

### Toxic-food close (required on chocolate, grape, chicken bone, gum and any later toxic-food episode)
- **Last spoken line, last caption line and description:** quote the ASPCA Animal Poison Control page, keeping its condition: *"If you think your pet may have ingested a potentially poisonous substance, call (888) 426-4435. A consultation fee may apply."*
- **Source:** ASPCA Animal Poison Control, https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control (wording checked Nov 16, 2026).
- **Optional:** add "or call your vet."
- **Don't add** doses, symptoms or timelines unless the episode's own fact pack sources them.

### YouTube tags (Studio "Tags" field, about 8–12)
```
{exact question}, {question variant 1 from autocomplete}, {question variant 2},
{species} {body part}, inside a {species}'s {body part}, {species} anatomy animated,
{species} pov, {species} facts, meatball myths, animated {species}
```
Example (#1): `why do dogs eat grass, why do dogs eat grass and throw up, why does my dog eat grass, dog stomach, inside a dog's stomach, dog anatomy animated, dog pov, dog facts, meatball myths, animated dog`

### TikTok caption
```
{exact searched question, lowercase}? {1 emoji} {short tease, no new fact}
{one checked fact with its condition; clean gag (Meatball the human can be the butt of it); end on an open question?}
{toxic-food episodes only: the ASPCA close from the section above}
#{topic1} #{topic2} #{topic3} #{topic4} #{topic5}
```
- **Example (#1):** `#whydogseatgrass #dogseatgrass #dogstomach #dogfacts #dogbehavior`
- **Hashtag rules:** exactly 5 topic hashtags. No brand tag, no `#fyp`, no `#learnontiktok`.
- **Per-episode captions:** the captions in the §2 table are the search line only (line 1). Add the 5 topic tags at posting time.
- **Also on TikTok:**
  - Say the exact question as the first spoken line.
  - Show it as an on-screen search card for 2–2.5 s (bible hook rule).
  - Pin a comment with the same question.
- **Hashtag rule: decided** (Social Studio, Nov 16). TikTok follows MASTER_ORDERS: 5 topic tags, no brand tag. The current `postkit.py lint` `tt-` check already enforces this.

## Limits
- **No YouTube or TikTok search volumes.**
  - vidIQ's keyword data needs a login: `vidiq.com/youtube-keyword-tool/` redirects, and the keyword URLs I tried returned 404. No vidIQ numbers are used.
  - The only volumes here are 3P Google web numbers, and they're labeled that way.
- **Autocomplete is a ranked list, not a volume.**
  - It also shifts between calls: e.g. "why do dogs eat grass" came back #2 on both runs, but some lower ranks moved by 1–3.
  - Queries ran from the box with `gl=us` / `region=US`. They aren't personalized to our accounts.
- **No TikTok competitor videos.** The TikTok suggestion API was reachable. TikTok search results (videos and their view counts) weren't pulled, because there's no logged-out endpoint. TikTok evidence here is suggestions only.
- **View counts are yt-dlp search snapshots** from the evening of Nov 16 ET. Channel-tab counts (Organ Crew, Zoo Inside tab) are YouTube-rounded.
- **"Top direct video" is my pick** of the most-viewed result that answers the question. Higher-view off-topic results, like compilations and music, were skipped.
