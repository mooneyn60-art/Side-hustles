### 1. Most House Spiders Can Be Left Alone if You Identify Them 🕷️ (Is This Spider Venomous?)

**Format:** Multi-creature "don't squish / ID" episode, same family as "3 Bugs You Should Never Kill," not a born-as and not a cellar-spider remake.

**Statement title:** `Most House Spiders Can Be Left Alone if You Identify Them 🕷️ (Is This Spider Venomous?)`

- Statement form, no question mark in the core title. "Can" is the hedge. Creature and the searched idea ("house spiders") sit in the first 40 characters. One emoji. Series tag last. No banned words. No invented count of "3 and 2" until the fact pack locks which species belong in each pile.

**0:05 promise line:** "Most spiders in your house can be left alone, if you can tell them apart. Here are the ones to leave, and the ones to get identified."

**Target length:** 8–10 minutes.

**8-beat outline** (retention peak noted with *):

1. *Cold open (0:00–0:25).* Frame 0: a cellar spider already wrapping prey. Promise spoken by 0:05. Pay off immediately with one leave-alone tell (two body sections, web in a corner) versus one "get this identified" tell (violin or hourglass markings), no rules intro.
2. *Leave-alone 1: cellar spider.* The one in the corner that eats bigger spiders. One new fact not used as the whole 11/14 episode; point watch-next at that video rather than retelling it. Peak: the wrap.
3. Leave-alone 2: jumping spider at the window. Sourced leap ("up to 40 body lengths" only if the Missouri Conservationist line in the existing Short still holds on a live check). Peak: the jump.
4. Leave-alone 3: wolf spider or giant house spider, one ID tell each (no web vs. 4-inch-class leg span — any span number needs a live source). Peak: the run or the size card.
5. **Halfway re-hook + the only comment prompt:** "Which of these is in your house right now? Drop the name, or describe it, in the comments." Then: "The next two are the ones you should not guess from a photo."
6. *Identify, don't guess: false widow vs. black widow.* Side-by-side tells. Peak: the two markings on screen at once. Safety line with its condition (beat 8).
7. Identify: brown recluse vs. common lookalikes. "Most bites don't rot skin" stays hedged; no invented medical percentages. Peak: the violin tell, then the lookalikes.
8. *What to do, then verdict.* How to get a spider identified (extension / local authority, **needs the exact path from a live official page**). Safety. Callback to the opening wrap. Watch-next: the 11/14 cellar-spider long-form, or the 11/21 centipede if that is still the newest house episode.

**Fact sources it needs:**

- Existing: `research/76-cellar-spider-factpack.md` (UC Riverside Vetter, Burke Museum Crawford, Penn State Extension, UC IPM, Australian Museum, Zobel-Thropp et al. 2019 — species limits already flagged in the pack). Wolf-spider, giant-house-spider, false-widow, and recluse Shorts / hook-tests already cite extension and museum pages in their JSON `sources` fields; the pack must reopen those live pages, not copy the Shorts.
- Pull live: CDC "venomous spiders" (or current equivalent) for medical-importance wording; UC IPM Spiders; university extension ID sheets for each species kept in the script. No "most venomous" rumor, and no hospital claim that the pack cannot quote.

**Why it should work:** This is the format that has actually made us hours. "3 Bugs" is the only Meatball long-form that clears the 72-hour bars (5.44% CTR, 42.71% viewed, 4.02 hours). The two later episodes in the same family still clear 4%+ CTR. ### 2. Your Life as a Cockroach: 8 Things You Have to Get Through

**Format:** Born-as, house-bug subject. This is the shape bet applied to the house-bug topic that already makes us hours.

**Target length:** 8–10 minutes (lion v2 house: about 8:10 of speech plus a 12 s end screen).

**8-beat outline** (each beat is one of the eight, after a 0:00–0:25 cold open that already states the promise; the halfway comment sits between 4 and 5):

1. *The egg case.* You start in a case your mother has been carrying. Peak: the hatch. (UC IPM Pest Notes 7467 already used on the Shorts says a German cockroach egg case holds about 30 young — use that wording, not the Short title "40 Babies," until a live check confirms a number.)
2. *The crack.* You can flatten into a gap on the order of a dime or a couple of pennies — only the figure the live Berkeley / PNAS 2016 source still supports. Peak: the squeeze.
3. *The kitchen at night.* You are nocturnal and you follow food and water. Peak: lights-out kitchen.
4. *How fast a kitchen gets crowded.* Offspring counts only as a range, with the condition (one female and her offspring, German cockroach, under stated conditions) from the live UC IPM page. Do not say "30K in your kitchen" unless the pack can quote it. Peak: the multiplier card.

6. *What actually hurts you.* Temperature, dryness, or bait — only what extension / EPA pages state, with the condition. Peak: the failed raid.
7. *The headless rumor, told straight.* A cockroach can live for a time without its head, as the Scientific American / entomologist wording already used in the Short; the pack must reopen that page and keep the time hedge ("for weeks" only if the source still says it). Peak: the twist, then the plain limit.

**Head start:** Medium-high. Four roach Shorts plus explainer animation. No life-cycle fact pack yet.

**Effort:** Medium (new pack, new eight-beat script, mostly existing rig). **Payoff vs. effort: 2 of 6.**
