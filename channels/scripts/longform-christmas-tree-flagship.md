## 3 to 8. The ones you can ignore (STOWAWAYS 3 TO 8)
- Three. Spiders. Iowa State says aphids and spiders are the two things most often found on cut trees.  `F14`
- Penn State says they aren't dangerous to you or your pets, and they die in a short time indoors.  `F72`
- Four. Bark beetles. You might see a little very fine sawdust.  `F66`

- Five. White cottony fluff on the bark. Adelgids. They stay put and they're harmless.  `F69`

- Seven. Mites. Penn State says none of the predatory mites on Christmas trees bite.  `F68`
- But if there's an old bird nest in the tree, take it out. Nests can carry mites and lice.  `F73`
- Eight. Bark lice. They can't bite, and dry air kills them quickly.  `F67`
- And the two scales? Penn State says they don't have time to grow up indoors. At worst, a little sticky honeydew.  `F71`
- Iowa State sums up the tiny ones. They can't bite or sting, and they won't live long enough to grow or multiply.  `F20`
- Newly hatched aphids and spiderlings are about a sixteenth of an inch.  `F22`
- Most of them starve or dry out.  `F21`
- Your living room is the worst resort they've ever booked. Dry, foodless, terrible.  `joke`
- Penn State's rule for anything you find: leave it on the tree until the tree goes out, and vacuum whatever lands on the walls or ceiling.  `F75`

## 9. The one that matters outside (STOWAWAY 9 OF 9)
- Nine. This one is not a problem in your house. It's a problem for your county.  ``
- Spotted lanternfly eggs. Penn State calls it unlikely, but they can turn up on Christmas trees.  `F74; Penn State Extension, Insects on Real Christmas Trees (updated 2026-01-19); Penn State News 2019-11-18`
- On bark, an egg mass can look like a smear of dried mud.  `Penn State News 2019-11-18 ("resemble gray mud splatters"); Penn State SLF Management Guide ("mud-like covering"); USDA APHIS`
- If they hatch in your living room, the babies soon die.  `F74`
- The worry is the ride. Eggs on a tree could reach new ground and hatch outside in spring.  `Virginia Cooperative Extension ENTO-402; Penn State Christmas-tree page`
- So if you live in or near a lanternfly area, check the trunk before the tree travels.  `Penn State Christmas-tree page; Penn State SLF Management Guide; New Jersey Dept. of Agriculture`
- Found one? Scrape it into rubbing alcohol or hand sanitizer, or smash it.  `Penn State News 2019-11-18; Penn State SLF Management Guide; USDA APHIS`
- Outside the known lanternfly area? Report it to your state agriculture department.  `Penn State SLF Management Guide; USDA APHIS; Penn State Christmas-tree page`
- And in a lanternfly area, send the old tree to a chipping program, not the backyard.  `Penn State Christmas-tree page`

## The tree check (THE TREE CHECK)
- Here is the whole tree check.  ``
- One. Before it comes inside, look up into the tree from the cut end, and scan the branches for the marshmallow.  `F78 F39`
- Two. Clip any egg case, twig and all, and tuck it into an evergreen outside.  `F40`
- Three. Shake the tree. Some lots have a shaker. Then move it away from that spot, or the bugs can crawl back.  `F76 F77`
- Four. Pull out any old bird nest, and check the trunk.  `F73 F74`
- Five. Once it's inside: vacuum, never squash, never spray.  `F59 F60 F52`
- That's it.  ``
