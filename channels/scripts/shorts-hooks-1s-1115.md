## How each hook is built

- First spoken line: about 6 words, voice from 0.00 s, search keyword plus the payoff, no rules intro, no countdown, no spoken study name, year, or sample size.
- Frame 0: the animal already moving; an on-screen card delivers the payoff in under 1 s. Meatball still hops in at about 2.3 s. Line 2 (3–7 s) stays the Meatball gag.
- Last spoken line ends on "And" and runs grammatically into the first spoken line. The full loop sentence is shown.
- Hedges stay (`about`, `typically`, `can`, `may`, `up to`, `one`, `in one study`). Sources move to the on-screen tag or the description, not the voice.
- Safety or action lines are said clean, with the condition still attached, later in the Short, never as the hook and never as the joke.
- No banned words: TRUE, FALSE, Myth, Believe, Cartoons, Lied, "Did you know".
- YouTube: mild words only, at most one per Short, never in the hook. Jokes stay on Meatball and add no number, danger, feeling, motive, or instruction.

## 1. Mon 11/16/2026 16:00 ET — `dust-mites`

Searched question (packaging / kw): "Do dust mites live in my bed?"

Current spoken hook in `dust-mites-v6-yt.json`: "Your bed's female mites lay about 50 eggs."

**1-second hook (6 words):** "Female dust mites lay about 50 eggs."  
_Scriptwriter edit: swapped with the alternate. "Dust mites in bed: about 50 eggs" could be heard as 50 eggs per bed, so the spoken line now names the female._

The extra word over a 6-word target is the hedge "about," which stays. Search keywords are "dust mites" and "bed." Payoff is the egg count.

**Frame 0:** a dust mite already crawling on bed fibers or a sheet, close-up, moving. Meatball is not in the frame yet.

On-screen card (under 1 s):

```
ABOUT 50 EGGS
FROM ONE FEMALE
DUST MITES IN YOUR BED
```

Source tag (card or description, not spoken): University of Kentucky Entomology, House Dust Mites (ENTFACT-646).

**Alternate hook:** "Dust mites in bed: each female, about 50 eggs."

**Loop-back ending**

Last line: "They stay most numerous in beds. And"

Full loop sentence: "They stay most numerous in beds. And female dust mites lay about 50 eggs."

Second sourced fact, later, not in the hook (same UKY page): under ideal conditions they grow from egg to adult in about a month; adults live about 1–2 months; humidity below 50 percent makes survival difficult; they tend to be most numerous in beds, overstuffed sofas and chairs, and adjacent carpeted areas.

**Hedges kept:** "about 50"; "about a month"; "about 1–2 months"; "under ideal conditions."

**Safety / condition line:** this is not a wildlife-safety Short and it is not a treatment guide. If the humidity fact is kept, say it clean, with the condition: "Humidity below 50 percent makes survival difficult." Do not promote a "keep humidity under 50 percent" line as a cure, and do not invent a mite count for a viewer's bed.

**Joke (existing line 2, after Meatball hops in):** "Meatball hasn't washed his damn sheets since March." On Meatball only. "Damn" is the one mild word. It adds no mite number, danger, feeling, motive, or instruction.

**Sources used:** UKY ENTFACT-646, already in the v6 spec and in `bug-shorts-1114-1120.md`.

## Flags: 1-second payoff vs MASTER_ORDERS

### 1. First spoken line is the searched question

MASTER_ORDERS (engagement rule 1, and "Tags and everything"): the hook is the searched question, and the searched phrase is said in the first spoken sentence.

A full searched question often uses 6–10 words and still withholds the payoff ("Do cats always land on their feet?", "What to do if a black bear attacks you?"). That fights Packaging's 1-second rule.

**How to satisfy both:** the first spoken line is a compact fusion of the search keyword or short question plus the payoff, about 6 words, no preamble. The full searched question stays in the title, in the first sentence of the description, and on the frame-0 card (or a keyword card in seconds 1–4, which the v6 template already asks for). Do not open on a bare question that hides the twist, and do not open on a study name.

Where the fusion ran over 6 words (dust mites, carpet beetles, Christmas tree, cat-falls), the extra words are hedges or the 46.2% figure. The card still shows the payoff in under 1 s.

### 2. Meatball hops in at about 2.3 s

The v6 template keeps frame 0 as the animal close-up already moving, with Meatball hopping in at about 2.3 s, and line 2 (3–7 s) as the Meatball gag.

**How to satisfy both:** do not wait for Meatball to start the Short. Voice and the payoff card start at 0.00 s on the moving animal. Meatball's entrance and the gag stay at 2.3 s and line 2. The joke never carries the hook fact.

### 3. "You" + number hook vs a 6-word cap

The v6 template also wants a visceral "you" + number hook. "Your bed" / "your tree" / "your house" can blow the word cap.

**How to satisfy both:** put "you / your / your bed / your tree" on the card and in later lines. The spoken hook keeps the number (or the rule) and the search keyword. Cat-falls and dust-mites still aim the Short at the viewer's pet or house; they just do not spend the first second on "your."

### 4. Spoken citations vs sourced facts

Packaging's 11/15 review: study names, years, and sample sizes read aloud kill momentum. MASTER_ORDERS and the checker still require sourced facts, and a percentage needs what it is a share of.

**How to satisfy both:** keep every hedge and condition. Move author, year, journal, and sample size to the on-screen source tag and the description Sources line. In the voice, say "in one study," "one saltwater croc," "typically," "about," not "Vnuk et al. 2004, 119 cats" or "Erickson, PLoS ONE 2012."

### 5. Safety Shorts with long searched questions

Not used as one of the five, but the same conflict is sharper on `mountain-lion` (11/27), `black-bear` (11/28), and `wolf-distance` (11/29). Those searched questions are long, and the payoff is a rule with a condition.

A black-bear fusion that keeps the species condition in the voice: "Black bear attacks: do not play dead." Card in under 1 s: "IF A BLACK BEAR ATTACKS / DO NOT PLAY DEAD / TRY TO REACH A CAR OR BUILDING." The later safety line is said clean, with the condition still attached: "If a black bear attacks, do not play dead. Try to get to a car or building, and if you cannot, fight back at the face and muzzle." Do not say "play dead" without naming the species, and do not say fight any bear (NPS, `95-topic-queue.csv` slug `black-bear`).
