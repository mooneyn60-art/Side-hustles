## Sources (opened 11/18, quotes copied from the pages)

- **SM21**: Sender R, Milo R. The distribution of cellular turnover in the human body. Nat Med 27:45-48 (2021), Weizmann Institute, doi:10.1038/s41591-020-01182-9, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33432173/ : 'In terms of cell numbers, close to 90% of the (0.33 +/- 0.02) x 10^12 cells per day turnover was blood cells.' Body text: 'a reference 70 kg person with 30 trillion host cells ... produces this number of cells every 80 d ... not all parts of the human body are ever replaced (for example, most brain neurons and cells of the eye lens ...) and hence complete turnover never happens.'
- **SFM16**: Sender R, Fuchs S, Milo R. Revised Estimates for the Number of Human and Bacteria Cells in the Body. PLoS Biol 14:e1002533 (2016), Weizmann Institute, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27541692/ : 'For human cells, we identify the dominant role of the hematopoietic lineage to the total count (~90%) and revise past estimates to 3.0x10^13 human cells.'
- **SP05**: Spalding KL et al. Retrospective birth dating of cells in humans. Cell 122:133-143 (2005), Karolinska Institute, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16009139/ : 'whereas nonneuronal cells are exchanged, occipital neurons are as old as the individual'
- **PLU**: Plutarch, Life of Theseus (Dryden tr.), https://classics.mit.edu/Plutarch/theseus.html : 'The ship wherein Theseus and the youth of Athens returned had thirty oars, and was preserved by the Athenians ... for they took away the old planks as they decayed, putting in new and stronger timber in their place, insomuch that this ship became a standing example among the philosophers ... one side holding that the ship remained the same, and the other contending that it was not the same.'
- **SEP-IT**: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 'Identity Over Time' (rev. Jan 12 2026), https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-time/ : 'Over a long period all of the planks composing a certain ship are replaced one by one. Eventually a ship indiscernible from the original, but composed of entirely different planks, results.'
- **SEP-MC**: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 'Material Constitution', https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/material-constitution/ : 'Thomas Hobbes imagined a scenario in which a custodian collects the original planks and puts them back in the original arrangement. ... we are left with two seafaring vessels ... But where is the original Ship of Theseus? Some will say that it is with the museum, since boats can survive the gradual replacement of parts. Others will say that it is with the custodian, since constructs can survive being taken apart and put back together. Both arguments seem compelling, but together they imply that the ship survives twice over.'

Note: the SEP "Identity Over Time" entry (current revision, Jan 12 2026) describes the replace-every-plank and reassembly ship (it calls them Replacement and Reassembly) but **does not name Plutarch or Hobbes**. The Plutarch and Hobbes attributions are sourced to SEP "Material Constitution" and to Plutarch's own text instead.

Hook claim check: by cell count, about 90% of the 30 trillion human cells are blood-line cells (Sender, Fuchs & Milo, PLoS Biol 2016, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27541692/: "we identify the dominant role of the hematopoietic lineage to the total count (≈90%) and revise past estimates to 3.0·10^13 human cells"), and the body makes its own cell count's worth of new cells about every 80 days (SM21). So "most of your body's cells get swapped out over the years" holds by number. By mass it is slower (SM21: the cellular mass turns over about every year and a half; fat and muscle cells live over 10 years on average), and "complete turnover never happens" (SM21). The script never says every cell.
