# Fact pack: "Your Life as a Dust Mite: 8 Things You Have to Get Through" (dust-mite born-as long-form)

Prepared for pitch 3 in `/workspace/drafts/longform-pipeline-pitch-1117.md` and Packaging title claims in `/workspace/packaging/longform-bug-1-4-titles-thumbs-1117.md`. Research date 2026-11-15 (ET). Row IDs DM1 to DM38. Format follows the crocodile / cockroach packs: ID | fact | exact quote | URL | scope and how it was opened | do not say.

**Lead species: North American house dust mite, *Dermatophagoides farinae*.** UKY ENTFACT-646 names two common U.S. species: the North American house dust mite, *D. farinae*, and the European house dust mite, *D. pteronyssinus*. *D. farinae* is the named U.S. lead. The life-cycle and habit figures used here (about 50 eggs; adults approximately 1–2 months; egg-to-adult about one month when temperature and humidity are optimum; “cannot survive well” below 50% RH; most numerous in beds, sofas, chairs, and adjacent carpet) are stated on that page for **house dust mites**, not as *farinae*-only counts. Do not invent *farinae*-only numbers. Do not mix these mites with *Demodex* face mites (a different animal; see the report note, not a row in this pack).

## 0. Read this first
- The pitch-locked wash line always carries Mayo's **"at least 130°F (54.4°C)"**. The live dryer alternative is **at least 15 minutes** at a temperature **above 130°F**, then wash and dry to remove allergens — not the Packaging draft's 20 minutes. American Lung Association's "at least 120 degrees F" is a disagreement, not a blend (D1).
- "Females lay about 50 eggs" and "mites tend to be most numerous in beds…" are **separate** UKY sentences. Do not merge them into "lay 50 eggs in your bed."
- Humidity: prefer the live UKY line **"Dust mites cannot survive well at relative humidities below 50 percent"** (Title 3C). Also on the same page: "have a difficult time surviving when the relative humidity is below 50 percent," and some species can survive 50–60% RH. Keep "cannot survive well," not "all die at 50%."
- Do not mix mite heat numbers with bed-bug heat numbers. Mite wash/dryer: at least 130°F; dryer at least 15 minutes above 130°F (Mayo). Bed-bug dryer: high temperatures for **30 minutes**. Bed-bug article/area heat: **at least 120°F for 90 minutes**, often with a heat-generating device (EPA IPM). EPA bed-bug **steam** "at least 130°F" is a third, different method. Never swap them.
- Allergy wording stays hedged: can trigger symptoms in people who are allergic, not in everyone. Do not blend UKY "at least 45 percent of young people with asthma," ALA "Up to 85% of individuals with asthma," and National Jewish "children allergic to dust mites are five times more likely to develop asthma."
- No spoken citations. Each line carries its source in an on-screen tag. No medical advice beyond what the cited page says to do with bedding.
- Maine's extracted "16 hundredths of an inch" is not used (D5). Prefer UKY "about 0.5 mm."

## Hook numbers

Sourced "you + your home + a number or threat" lines. Hedges stay attached.

1. **At least 130°F can kill the mites in your bedding.** Mayo: wash in hot water that is at least 130 F to kill dust mites and remove allergens (DM20). Hedges: "at least"; sheets/blankets/pillowcases/bedcovers; kill and remove allergens. Dryer alternative: at least 15 minutes above 130 F, then wash and dry (DM21). Short hook: "A 130°F wash can kill mites in your bedding" only if "at least" stays in the voice or on the card.

2. **Females lay about 50 eggs.** UKY (DM18). Hedges: "about"; house dust mites, not a named *farinae*-only count; do not add "in your bed." Short hook: "A female dust mite lays about 50 eggs." (9 words)

3. **Dust mites cannot survive well below 50% humidity.** UKY (DM12). Hedges: "cannot survive well," not "all die"; some species can survive 50–60% RH. Mayo/ALA: keep the home below 50% (DM14–DM15). Short hook: "Dust mites cannot survive well below 50% humidity."

4. **An infested mattress can contain millions of dust mites** (DM19), or, as a home-frequency hook, **about 4 out of 5 U.S. homes have dust mite allergens in at least one bed** (DM5, ALA). Hedges: "infested" / "can contain"; "about 4 out of 5"; "allergens," not "mites crawling in every bed." Close fifth: thousands of mites in a single gram of house dust, a gram about the weight of a paperclip (DM19).
