## 0. Read this first
- The 100-foot line always carries the second half: they **tend to live within 8 feet of where people sleep** (CDC). "Can" is the hedge on 100 feet. The pitch promise's "usually live within 8 feet of the bed" should be tightened to the CDC wording ("tend to," "where people sleep"), not "usually" / "of the bed."
- Bite delay: CDC says marks "**may take as long as 14 days** to develop **in some people**," not Packaging's "can take up to 14 days" unless that is clearly the same hedge. Prefer the live CDC sentence.
- Heat: live EPA IPM (last updated March 30, 2026): dryer at **high temperatures for 30 minutes** (just washing will generally not kill bed bugs); heat infested articles and/or areas to **at least 120°F (about 49°C) for 90 minutes** to ensure eggs are killed; often a heat-generating device or special unit. EPA DIY (last updated August 10, 2026): **Do not try to kill bed bugs by increasing your indoor temperature with a thermostat, propane space heater, or fireplace — this does not work and is dangerous.** Whole-house heat is a professional method on that page, not a DIY thermostat job.
- Do not mix these with dust-mite numbers: mite wash/dryer is at least 130°F and at least 15 minutes above 130°F (Mayo). EPA bed-bug **steam** "at least 130°F" is a third, different method (BB28).
- "Never use a pesticide indoors that is intended for outdoor use." Follow the label; the product must list bed bugs. Foggers only with extreme care, and not as the only method.
- This episode is not a Night Shift remake. Tease hitchhiking and the 100-foot / 8-foot condition, not "what crawls at 4 a.m."
- Do not repeat the CDC page typo "loss off sleep." Say "loss of sleep" if that fact is used, or quote the typo only inside quotation marks.
- No spoken citations. Safety lines keep their official triggers.

## Warnings (unsafe to imitate / do not advise)

| ID | Warning |
|---|---|
| W1 | Do not try to kill bed bugs by raising indoor temperature with a thermostat, propane space heater, or fireplace. EPA: this does not work and is dangerous. Whole-house heat is a professional method, not a DIY thermostat job. (BB23, BB24) |
| W2 | Never use a pesticide indoors that is intended for outdoor use. Follow the label; the product must list bed bugs. (BB29, BB30) |

## Hook numbers

Sourced "you + your home + a number or threat" lines. The 100-foot hook always carries the 8-foot condition.

1. **Bed bugs can travel over 100 feet in a night, but tend to live within 8 feet of where people sleep.** CDC (BB1). Hedges: "can," "over," "in a night," "tend to," "where people sleep" (not "usually" / "of the bed"). The 8-foot half must be spoken by 0:05 in the pitch. Short title may use the 100-foot hook; the promise and script keep both halves.

2. **They tend to live within 8 feet of where you sleep.** Same CDC sentence (BB2). Hedge: "tend to." Do not say they never go farther (that contradicts hook 1).

3. **Bite marks may take as long as 14 days to develop in some people.** CDC (BB18). Hedges: "may," "as long as," "in some people." Prefer this over "can take up to 14 days."

4. **They can fit into a crevice no wider than a toothpick** (BB7, UKY), or **a high-heat dryer for 30 minutes can kill them on bedding and clothing** (BB21, EPA; just washing will generally not). Hedges: "no wider than a toothpick"; dryer "high temperatures," "30 minutes," bedding and clothing — not a mite 15-minute cycle, and not a whole-house thermostat.
