## Pre-sourced facts (all live this run)

### P5-F1. Fossils date to 400 million years ago — about twice as long ago as the dinosaurs (the new teaser payoff)

- Missouri Department of Conservation, Silverfish (Firebrat) field guide, https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/silverfish-firebrat : "They and their relatives the bristletails represent some of the most ancestral (primitive) insects and some of the first animals to walk on land. Their fossils date to 400 million years ago — about twice as long ago as the dinosaurs."
- University of Maryland Extension, Silverfish and Firebrats (updated Oct 17, 2024), https://extension.umd.edu/resource/silverfish-and-firebrats : "Silverfish and firebrats belong to one of the most primitive groups of insects. They have been identified from fossils dating back over 400 million years."
- Script-safe: "Its fossils date to 400 million years ago. That's about twice as long ago as the dinosaurs."
- Keep "about" and "fossils date to." Do not say it is twice as old as dinosaurs without "about." Do not reuse the old title. Skip "first animals to walk on land" unless a second source is opened for that clause (MDC only).

### P5-F2. They do not transmit disease

- MDC (same page): "Common silverfish and firebrats are indoor pests, damaging paper, books, wallpaper, fabrics, and foodstuffs. They do not transmit disease, however."
- Script-safe: "They do not transmit disease."
- One official government page; it is authoritative. Do not upgrade this to "harmless," "never bite," or "safe to handle."

### P5-F3. Mating dance, then a sperm capsule the female picks up

- University of California IPM, Pest Notes: Silverfish and Firebrats, https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/silverfish-and-firebrats/ : "Adults have an interesting courtship in which males attract females with a mating dance that includes antennae contact and head wagging, after which the male deposits a protected capsule containing sperm for the female to pick up."
- MDC: "After an elaborate mating ritual, female silverfish lay eggs in protected crevices."
- NC State ENT 425, Order Zygentoma, https://genent.cals.ncsu.edu/insect-identification/order-zygentoma/ : "Silverfish have an elaborate courtship ritual to insure exchange of sperm. The male spins a silken thread between the substrate and a vertical object. He deposits a sperm packet (spermatophore) beneath this thread and then coaxes a female to walk under the thread."
- University of Arkansas Arthropod Museum, Silverfish, https://arthropod.uark.edu/silverfish/ : "Thysanurans display complex courtship behavior. The sexes do not engage in copulation, and sperm transfer takes place externally and indirectly."
- Script-safe: "Males attract females with a mating dance — antennae contact and head wagging — then leave a sperm capsule for her to pick up."
- Keep "mating dance," "capsule," and "pick up." Do not say they mate the way mammals do. The silk-thread detail (NCSU) is optional extra, not required.

### P5-F4. Hatchlings look like the adults, only whitish and much smaller, with no scales at first

- MDC: "The hatchlings look like the adults, only whitish and much smaller. They go through several molts as they grow but never gain wings (much less pupate)."
- UC IPM: "Newly hatched nymphs are about 1/16 of an inch long, plump, white, and have no scales. They acquire their scales about 12 days after their fourth molt; at that point the firebrat and silverfish look like smaller versions of the adults"
- Penn State Extension, Bristletails (Silverfish and Firebrats), https://extension.psu.edu/bristletails-silverfish-and-firebrats (updated June 24, 2026): "The immature bristletails look similar to the adults, but do not have scales until after several molts."
- Script-safe: "The babies look like the adults, only whitish and much smaller, and they have no scales at first."
- "About 1/16 of an inch" and "about 12 days after their fourth molt" may be used only with those hedges, and only as UC IPM states them.

### P5-F5. They pass through 45 to 60 stages, or instars, in a life

- UC IPM: "Unlike other insects, firebrats and silverfish molt continuously throughout their life, passing through 45 to 60 instars or development stages."
- UF/IFAS EDIS IN1211 (PDF), https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN1211/pdf : "Once in its adult stage, the silverfish does not have a fixed number of instars (DeVries and Appel 2013), but has been recorded molting between 25 to 66 times in a lifespan (Richards and Davies 1977)."
- Script-safe: "It can pass through 45 to 60 stages, or instars, in its life."
- The "keeps molting its whole life" line already ran in the 10/28 TikTok. Use the **number**, not that punchline again. "Can" / "passing through 45 to 60" stays. Do not flatten UF's 25-to-66 "recorded" range into a single count.

### P5-F6. Silverfish live about 3 years; in some species, individuals can live for 8 years

- UC IPM: "Firebrats live about 2 years and silverfish about 3 years."
- Penn State: "Silverfish can live for at least two to three years and produce more than 50 offspring."
- UMD Extension: "The maximum lifespan in good conditions is 2 to 3 years."
- MDC: "In some species, individuals can live for 8 years and molt dozens of times."
- NC State: "Zygentomans may be rather long-lived — three years is probably typical and up to seven or eight years may be possible."
- Script-safe: "Silverfish live about 3 years. In some species, individuals can live for 8 years."
- Do not say "they live 8 years" or "maximum 8 years." UMD's "maximum… 2 to 3 years" and MDC's "in some species… 8 years" both stay hedged if the 8-year figure is used. NC State's "probably typical" / "may be possible" is the safest wording if you want both numbers in one sentence.

### P5-F7. They move rapidly, including sideways

- Penn State: "Both species move rapidly (including sideways), and their flat bodies let them hide in narrow crevices."
- MDC: "They run quickly on their six legs."
- University of Arkansas Arthropod Museum: "They run rapidly and hide to escape predators"
- Script-safe: "They move rapidly, including sideways, and their flat bodies let them hide in narrow crevices."
- Keep "including sideways." Do not add a speed number; none of these pages give one.

### P5-F8. Very weak jaws: they scrape, instead of bite, the surface of paper (good early scare-question beat)

- UC IPM, In Brief and Damage: "Firebrats and silverfish have very weak jaws which causes them to scrape, instead of bite, the surface of paper." And: "Firebrats and silverfish have very weak mandibles, or jaws, which causes them to scrape, instead of bite, the surface of paper."
- Script-safe: "Will it bite you? It has very weak jaws, so it scrapes the surface of paper instead of biting it."
- The source is about paper, not human skin. Do not write "never bite," "can't bite you," or "harmless to people." If you need a people-health line, use F2 ("They do not transmit disease") as a separate sentence.

### P5-F9. If they fall in a bathtub or sink, they can't climb the slippery sides; they can't crawl up through the drain

- UC IPM: "Sometimes you'll see these pests in your bathtub or sink. Even though they can't crawl up through the drain, if they fall in they can't climb up the slippery sides to escape."
- Script-safe: "If they fall in a bathtub or sink, they can't climb up the slippery sides. They can't crawl up through the drain, either."
- Keep both conditions. Do not say they come up the drain. (The 11/15 house-centipede cut used a tub-trap fact for the centipede; this is a different animal and a different mechanism. Fine to use, just don't imply it's the same guest.)

### P5-F10. They can invade new homes from nearby wild areas as those areas dry out in summer, and can come in on lumber and wallboard

- UC IPM: "Large numbers of these insects can invade new homes from surrounding wild areas, especially as these areas dry out during the summer. They also can come in on lumber, wallboard, and similar products."
- Script-safe: "They can invade new homes from wild areas nearby, especially as those areas dry out in summer. They can also come in on lumber and wallboard."
- Keep "can," "especially," and "similar products" if you mention the second sentence. Do not say every new house gets them.

## Spares (sourced, not needed if the ten above fill 61–65 s)

- **F-S1. Gut enzymes digest cellulose.** UC IPM: "Both insects have enzymes in their gut that digest cellulose, and they choose book-cases, closets, and places where books, clothing, starch, or dry foods are available."
- **F-S2. Lost feelers or tails can grow back in two to four weeks.** UF/IFAS IN1211: "If lost, silverfish can regenerate terminal filaments and antennae in two to four weeks (Morita 1926)." Script-safe: "If it loses a feeler or a tail, it can grow it back in two to four weeks." One university page, citing a 1926 paper; keep "can" and the time range.
- **F-S3. Best at 72–81°F and 75–97% relative humidity.** UARK: "It prefers cool, damp situations, developing and reproducing best at 72-81°F and 75-97% relative humidity." (The cool/damp basement preference itself is already used; the numbers are new.)
- **F-S4. Dustlike scales around damaged items.** UC IPM: "If you see scales around or beneath damaged items, it is a good indication that these pests are the culprits. The scales are delicate, dustlike, and slightly incandescent in the light, and they stick to most surfaces."
- **F-S5. Size up to about 3/4 inch.** UC IPM: "The common silverfish, Lepisma saccharina, is shiny, silver or pearl gray, and about 1/2 inch long, although it can grow as long as 3/4 inch." MDC and UMD also say about ½ inch. Keep "about" and "can."

## Do not use, or use only with a hard hedge

- **"Older than dinosaurs is eating your books"** as title, hook, or thumbnail: already live.
- **House centipedes eat silverfish** as a main fact: already in Parts 1–4 and the 10/28 silverfish cut. One short series link is enough.
- **Months / a year without food:** already used as "months" in the 3D explainer. Sources also disagree (UC IPM "several months"; Penn State "weeks without food and water, and more than 300 days if water is available"; UF "up to an entire year"). Do not pick a single number.
- **Egg counts:** sources disagree (UC IPM average clutch of 50, range 1 to 200; Penn State "one to three at a time"; UF clutches of seven to twelve, "no more than 30 eggs at a time"). Cut a specific count unless the line names one source and its hedge.
- **"They live 8 years"** as a flat claim. See F6.
- **"They never bite you."** F8 is about paper. No opened page said silverfish never bite people.
- **"First animals to walk on land"** without a second source (MDC only).
- CAES fact sheet (https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/caes/documents/publications/fact_sheets/entomology/silverfish_firebrats_zygentoma.pdf) was re-opened (200) and matches the already-used 10/28 lines (starch, tissue-thin holes, 3 months to 2 years, lifelong molting, humidity, airtight storage). Do not mine it for "new" punchlines.
