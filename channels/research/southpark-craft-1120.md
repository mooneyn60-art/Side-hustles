# South Park craft research + Meatball cutout script formula (Studio, Fri 11/20/2026)
Asked by Nolan 11/20 ("if you have to do a 1 hour research session to get this right then do it"). Builds on `cutout-traction-1120.md` (open/length/targets) and `raunch-ceiling-1120.md` (raunch lives in the premise). Scripts written from this: `channels/scripts/cutout-formula-batch1-1120.md`.
**Method:** interviews and the documentary via press coverage; yt-dlp auto-captions (word-level timestamps) from 6 official South Park Shorts + 5 comp Shorts, pulled 11/20 (YouTube rate-limited the box, so the Haminations and Hatim's captions didn't come through: Hatim's has no captions because his Shorts have no dialogue, and Haminations returned errors on every retry). Our own pace comes from ffprobe on our renders. Anything without a link is labeled **GUESS**.

## TL;DR (what changes in our writing)
1. **Every beat joined by BUT or THEREFORE, never "and then".** Our old cutouts were "and then" joke lists (Rick DNA: reveal, reveal, reveal). Parker and Stone call that "pretty boring" ([NYU / mtvU *Stand In*, 2011](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGUNqq3jVLg)).
2. **Each scene is a mini-sketch with its own laugh, but it causes the next one** ("each individual scene has to work as a kind of funny sketch", same talk).
3. **Small, dumb, personal problem → bad fix → it goes societal → an absurd authority makes it worse → reversal → the lesson.** That's the house shape (sources in section 1).
4. **The moral is written last and is usually ironic or wrong** ([Vulture, Parker: "always ends up being the last thing we put in the show"](https://www.vulture.com/2011/09/south-park-lessons-matt-stone-trey-parker.html)).
5. **The official SP clips that went viral run ~2.4-3.5 words/s (avg ~2.9) and put the premise in line 1 (0-5 s).** The biggest one ("And it's gone", 15.6M) has a **~9 s dead-silent deadpan hold** after its turn. Comps that are new IP (MeatCanyon, Explosm, Hatim's) speak far less (0-1.8 w/s) and lean on the visual. Our voice runs ~2.55-3.0 w/s gross, so a 30 s cut holds **70-78 words**, max ~5 beats.
6. **For 30 s: one premise, one turn, one escalation, one reversal, one button.** That's the "Don't Say That, Billy" (15 s, 3 beats) and "And it's gone" (54 s, 5 beats) shape, compressed.

## 4. THE MEATBALL CUTOUT SCRIPT FORMULA
**Our measured pace (ffprobe on our renders, kokoro adam-puck E2):** ep8 Rick DNA 176 w / 62.1 s (2.83 w/s); Holy Black Friday v4 192 w / 64.4 s (2.98); ep3 v3 ~185 w / 62.1 s (~2.98); Black Friday v8 159 w / 62.4 s (2.55, heavy holds). **Use 2.9 w/s gross** (pauses and holds included).

### Before writing: the one-line chain
Write `X happens, BUT …, THEREFORE …, BUT …, THEREFORE [moral]` first. If any link reads "and then", fix the plot before writing jokes. It goes above every script.

### 30 s YouTube cut: 5 beats, 70-78 spoken words
| Time | Beat | Words | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0-2.5 s | **1. PREMISE** (the small, dumb, personal problem, with the named target or the news fact) | 5-12 | Frame 1 = target on screen + caps headline (clean). First line states the problem. No swear. |
| 2.5-8 s | **2. BUT: the objection / bad fix** (a character tries to escape the problem) | 10-16 | Conflict by 2 s. The fix must be a decision a character makes (THEREFORE). |
| 8-16 s | **3. THEREFORE: it goes societal** (the celebrity / institution / absurd authority shows up and makes it worse, using its real reported behavior) | 15-20 | The named-target joke lives here. The best "fact turned into a joke" line goes here. |
| 16-23 s | **4. BUT: the reversal** (the escape route loops back worse) + **1.0 s deadpan hold** | 10-18 | Everyone freezes for the hold; no music sting. |
| 23-28.5 s | **5. MORAL + BUTTON** (Meatball's lesson, ironic; the 1 swear lands on its last words) | 15-25 | The moral blames the system and the hypocrite, never a group. |
| 28.5-30 s | End card | 0 | Branding + comment prompt (+ "Follow for Ep. N" on series). |
Swears: **1** (max 2), after 7 s, on the punchline. Joke average 3.5+, nothing under 3.

### 65 s TikTok cut: same plot, 8-10 beats, 185-195 spoken words
| Time | Beat | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 s | 1. Premise (same frame 1 and first line as the YT cut) | 6-12 |
| 3-10 s | 2. BUT the objection, plus the hypocrite's first self-own | 15-25 |
| 10-20 s | 3. THEREFORE the bad fix #1 (the family's own solution; gross-out / spoken innuendo lives here) | 25-35 |
| 20-28 s | 4. BUT fix #1 fails, THEREFORE they escalate to the outside power | 15-25 |
| 28-42 s | 5. The named target / authority makes it worse: rule of three on its real reported behavior, third one hardest | 35-45 |
| 42-50 s | 6. BUT the reversal (crawl back, or the loophole backfires) | 15-25 |
| 50-51 s | 7. Deadpan hold 1.0 s | 0 |
| 51-60 s | 8. Moral (Meatball): ironic lesson ending on the f-word or the hardest swear | 25-35 |
| 60-63.5 s | 9. Post-moral button: 1-3 lines that undercut the moral (self-own) | 6-15 |
| 63.5-65 s | End card (1.5 s; stretch to 2.5 s if the render is under 61 s) | 0 |
Swears: **2-3** (1 f-word allowed), all after 7 s and on punchlines. Holds can be stretched to hit 61 s; never add filler.

### Joke checklist per line
Score 1-5 (QA checklist A); cut anything under 3. Each line is either a plot turn (BUT/THEREFORE) or a joke, ideally both. The joke types to reach for, in order: **fact turned against itself** (the real number, the real clause), **literalism**, **rule of three with the third hardest**, **absurd authority logic**, **self-own button**, **callback to an earlier episode**.

### Safety rails (unchanged, from the checklist and the 9:18/9:20/9:55/10:05 notes)
Named targets: behavior and public record only, never bodies, no invented crimes, no fake quotes presented as real (cartoon dialogue in an absurd scene is obviously fictional). Sex: spoken innuendo only, never shown, **never in a beat with Pip or Dot**. No slurs, no "group is lesser", no abuse jokes, no jokes on victims of ongoing tragedies (so no Ukraine-war jokes in the summit episode). Hit both US parties across the politics episodes.
