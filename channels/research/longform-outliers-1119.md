## 6. Five takeaways for our next flagship (4:00 to 4:45, one question)

Each ties to evidence above. The earlier files already set the title and topic; these are about the script and picture.

1. **Open on a scene, then ask the question and name three outcomes by 0:25.** Video A says what happens now at 0:17, then "trap her, chew out, or fix it" by 0:22. Video C opens on a bathroom action with no title card. Our flagship "House Bugs Ranked" opened on "BUG CAM / IT'S 2 AM" (Studio review), which is a good open loop, but it never named outcomes the video then answers. Write the three outcomes as three spoken questions and pay all three off at the end, in order, like A does at 6:58 to 7:08. [YT-DLP captions; STUDIO]
2. **Plan 6 to 7 re-hooks across 4:15, one every 40 to 45 seconds, each one a new question or a split.** A raises about 9 in 7:44 (one per 52 s) and its longest gap is about 60 s. B turns the story about every 45 s. At 4:15 (255 s) one per 40 s is 6 hooks. Use A's most reusable types: "Is he about to sting you?" (a fear check), "So, who's this?" (a twist on who is in the scene), "at least X can't happen, right?" (a false safe), and "So, is sealing ever the right move?" (a condition). [OUR MATH on captions]
3. **Keep the fast cuts we already have, and fix the repeat by making each return change.** All three videos cut at least every 2.6 to 6.2 s, so our 2 to 3 s is not a problem. A returns to one porch in 29% of frames and still got 37.5x, because the porch changes each time (broom, question mark, woodpecker, autumn, winter, check mark). So the Studio rule "at most 3 uses of any background" is stricter than the evidence needs; a better rule is "the home base may return, but something in it must be different each visit". This is my reading of 3 videos, not a tested rule. [OUR MATH on storyboards; STUDIO]
4. **Do not put a black card at the end. End on the opening scene with a callback line, and say the next video out loud.** A ends on the opening sawdust ("It was the newest few inches of a tunnel that may have been growing for years") and C ends back in the bathroom looking into the bowl. B holds a "NEXT EPISODE" card for about 10 s over a moving sunset and says the next title at 619.7 s. Our review found 25 s of black "WATCH NEXT" boxes with nothing in them. Fix: the last 15 s stay animated in the opening room, the callback line is spoken there, and one end-screen element goes over it. Whether A and C used YouTube's own end-screen elements is not measured; check in Studio. [YT-DLP captions; OUR MATH; STUDIO]
5. **Use an honest "nobody has measured this" beat as a mid-video stake.** A says "Nobody has run the experiment" at 3:06 and "Nobody has measured which" at 5:25, and uses the Ohio State and Georgia against Cornell split to hold viewers through the 2:37 to 4:33 stretch. It adds a one-line limit at 6:14 ("the bench was one close look, not a survey"). This fits our second-model check rule and gives us a re-hook that costs no new claim. If the flagship is a comedy with a second-person "life of..." shape instead, B's version of the same move is a one-line escalation after each beat ("You're a business model in sneakers") with 8 titled chapters in the description. B had 1,200 subscribers and still reached 150,090. The "Why It Sucks" title still needs Nolan's yes (MASTER_ORDERS open ask 2). [YT-DLP captions; OUR MATH]
