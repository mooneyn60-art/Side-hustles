## Part 1 (topics 11-15)

### 11. Creator Rewards: qualified-view rules and RPM ranges

**Sources:**
1. [TikTok Newsroom: Introducing the New Creator Rewards Program](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program/)
2. [TikTok Support: How rewards work](https://support.tiktok.com/fr/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program/how-rewards-work) (official Support page; French locale in the URL we retrieved)
3. [makeviral, “TikTok Creator Rewards Program: Rules, RPM, Pay 2026”](https://www.makeviral.ai/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program) — third-party RPM compilation, checked against pages dated August–September 2026

**Finding:** Official eligibility is: 18 or older, at least 10,000 followers, at least 100,000 views in the last 30 days, and a personal account in good standing in a country where the program is available. Eligible videos must be original and over one minute long. We have 9,316 followers, so we are not in the program yet.

TikTok Support says rewards start after 1,000 qualified views on the For You feed. Qualified views are unique For You-feed views. TikTok excludes fraudulent views, paid views, “not interested” / disliked views, views under 5 seconds, promoted views, and artificial views. Repeat views from the same account count once.

RPM is the average reward per 1,000 qualified views. Official RPM inputs are: video performance (average watch time and finish rate), search value (search traffic, including views on the search page), location of the creator and of the viewers, engagement, and advertising value (how long viewers watch ads). TikTok does not publish an official RPM table. Third-party compilations (Elev8or and Soundstripe, via makeviral, August–September 2026) report about $0.40 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views, with a US figure around $0.85. That range is not from TikTok. UNVERIFIED: any official RPM dollar range, and the third-party claim that search-origin views need 30 seconds of watch time to qualify (that 30-second rule did not appear on the official Support page we retrieved).

**Action for us:** Crossing 10,000 followers is the gate. Until then, none of our plays pay through Creator Rewards. Once we qualify, do not treat public plays as payable views: official qualified views are unique For You views over 5 seconds. Part 2’s 56.4% Search share can still help RPM through the official “search value” factor, but most of those search plays may not count as qualified views.

### 12. TikTok search: how keywords in speech and text get indexed

**Sources:**
1. [Search Engine Land: TikTok SEO](https://searchengineland.com/guide/tiktok-seo)
2. [Hootsuite: TikTok SEO](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-seo/)
3. Our own Part 2 analytics: 650,600 plays, 56.4% Search, 42.8% For You

**Finding:** Search Engine Land says TikTok uses video information to understand what a video is about, including spoken audio, on-screen textual elements (subtitles, titles, emojis), captions, and hashtags. It tells creators to put the main keyword in native on-screen text, say it in the audio (at least once, preferably near the start), put it in the caption and a hashtag, and use it in thumbnail text. Hootsuite says the same: say the keywords out loud, and put them in on-screen text, because TikTok processes spoken audio and can read on-screen text. SocialSeal’s caption-only study of 16,579 Latin-alphabet searches found 77% of top-20 videos shared at least one of the search’s words in the caption, but they could not check speech or on-screen text. UNVERIFIED: a public TikTok help page that names speech-to-text and on-screen OCR as formal search-index fields. The SEO guides are consistent, and they match how we already package videos.

Our own numbers support this more strongly than any guide: Part 2’s 650,600 plays were 56.4% Search. Search, not For You, carried that video.

**Action for us:** Keep the standing rule: the caption’s first line is the question people type, with the bug’s full name; say and show that keyword in the first 3 seconds; use 4–5 specific hashtags, no #fyp.

### 13. Series and playlists (Part 1/2/3): their effect on follows

**Sources:**
1. [Blossom: Do Part 2 TikToks Work? What the Series Data Shows](https://blossai.com/blog/part-2-series-content) — 459 series videos vs 51,125 typical videos in their library
2. Our own numbers: Part 2, 650,600 plays; Part 4, 293 plays

**Finding:** I did not find a TikTok-official study that series or playlists raise follow rate. Blossom’s library is the only numbered comparison in this pass: videos labeled as part of a series (“Part 1,” “Day 12”) reached 1.28 times the account’s usual views, while the typical video reached 2.26 times, so series posts landed about 43% lower on views. Their explanation is that Part 1 often withholds the ending, and Part 2 often opens with a recap that loses cold viewers. A series can still help the account, but only when each part stands on its own. Blog claims of “4x follow conversion” or “+520% profile visits” during a series were unsourced in the pages retrieved. UNVERIFIED: that Part 1/2/3 labels, or TikTok playlists, raise follows on their own.

Our own numbers show the same risk: Part 2 did 650,600 plays, Part 4 did 293. A series name did not carry the later part.

**Action for us:** Keep using parts when the question needs more than one video, but every part must open as a standalone search video (bug name and question in the first 3 seconds), not as a recap for people who already watched Part 1. “Follow for Part 2” is a weak close if Part 2 cannot win a cold For You or Search viewer on its own.

### 14. Best video length for Creator Rewards vs. completion rate

**Sources:**
1. [TikTok Newsroom: Introducing the New Creator Rewards Program](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program/)
2. [TikTok Support: How rewards work](https://support.tiktok.com/fr/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program/how-rewards-work)

**Finding:** Official rule: Creator Rewards only pays on original videos over one minute. The official formula’s “play duration” metric counts both watch time and finish rate, so a longer video only helps if people actually stay. Support also lists “well-produced” additional-reward criteria that include duration over one minute and 1080p or higher. There is no official sweet-spot length above that one-minute floor. Third-party completion tables (for example 31.4% completion at 61–90 seconds vs 22.7% over 90 seconds) were not accompanied by a method we could check. UNVERIFIED: any specific best length or completion-rate percentage above 60 seconds.

**Action for us:** Keep TikTok cuts at 61–90 seconds (the Editor’s 61–75s cuts already sit just over the floor) so they can enter Creator Rewards the day we pass 10,000 followers. Do not pad a tight question to 2–3 minutes unless the extra time is real content that people will finish.

### 15. Posting 3 a day vs. 1 a day: any data

**Sources:**
1. [Creafico, TikTok Benchmarks 2026](https://www.creafico.com/research/tiktok-benchmark-2026) — 24,373 public videos, July 25–October 22, 2026
2. [Buffer: How Often Should You Post on TikTok in 2026?](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/) — 11.4 million posts from over 150,000 accounts; fixed-effects regression
3. [PostOnce, posting-frequency statistics, updated 10 Nov 2026](https://postonce.to/posting-frequency-statistics) — self-comparison; TikTok line: when posting tripled, each video kept 89% of usual views

**Finding:** The datasets do not agree on per-video views, and the methods differ. Creafico’s cross-section (comparing different accounts) found 3+ posts a day had a median of 348 views per video, versus 3,222 for daily posters, but a higher expected count of 5x outliers per month (about 9 vs about 2). That dilution figure can be confounded by smaller or newer accounts posting more. Buffer compared each account with itself and found that posting more often was associated with more views per post, not fewer: up to 17% more at 2–5 posts a week, 29% at 6–10, and 34% at 11+ posts a week, versus once a week. The biggest efficiency jump was from 1 post a week to 2–5 a week. PostOnce’s self-comparison found that when TikTok creators tripled posting in a week, each video still got 89% of its usual views. UNVERIFIED: that 3 a day will raise or lower our median plays; none of these studies is our account.

Our own spread already dwarfs cadence effects: Part 2 at 650,600 plays vs Part 4 at 293. Search fit and whether the part stands alone matter more than 3 vs 1.

**Action for us:** Keep the locked 3-a-day volume. Judge it on our own median plays and follow rate after a full week, not on Creafico’s 348-view median alone. If later slots keep landing near Part 4’s 293 plays, put cadence on the Saturday proposal list with those numbers.

### 16. Hook formats that work in the first 1-3 seconds for educational content

**Sources:**
1. [Blossom: Educational TikTok Hooks That Reach Furthest](https://blossai.com/blog/education-hook-benchmarks)
2. Our own Part 2 analytics: 650,600 plays, 56.4% Search, 42.8% For You

**Finding:** In Blossom’s education library, the openings that reached furthest showed the thing working, or put a bold claim on screen, before explaining anything. Educational videos that opened on a live tech demo reached 14.02 times the account’s usual views (79 videos). Videos that opened with a provocative text claim reached 14.0 times (83 videos). A visual demonstration (the action happening in the first seconds) reached 11.92 times (49 videos). A utility promise (“here is what you will learn”) reached 7.04 times (225 videos). Keyword openers reached 0.93 times (196 videos) and generic openings 0.80 times (170 videos). Their checklist: something happens in second one, or a claim is on screen; cut the greeting; deliver a payoff within 5 seconds.

Blossom’s “times usual views” measure is an account-average comparison, not a search-share study. Our Part 2 (650,600 plays, 56.4% Search) still supports saying and showing the bug name and the question in the first 3 seconds for search. A bare keyword line, with no claim or visual result, is the weak education opener in their table.

**Action for us:** Keep the standing search rule (say and show the bug’s full name and the question in the first 3 seconds). Add a bold on-screen claim or a visual result in those same 3 seconds (for example a myth correction, or the bug on screen) so the hook can work on For You as well as Search. Do not open with a greeting or a delayed “in this video you’ll learn.”

### 17. Comment-reply videos as a growth tool

**Sources:**
1. [TikTok Newsroom: Product tutorial: Reply to comments with video](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/product-tutorial-reply-to-comments-with-video) (June 18, 2020)

**Finding:** The official feature lets you reply to a comment with a new video: the comment can be attached as a sticker, the reply is published in the comments section, and it is also posted as a new video on your page. At launch, TikTok said #replytocomments had more than 1.8 billion views. That is a 2020 product announcement, not a 2026 performance study. UNVERIFIED: any current average lift (including “41% higher engagement” figures that appeared only in secondary write-ups, not in a method we could check).

**Action for us:** Use comment-reply videos for real viewer questions on winners (especially Part 2), so the next video is already an asked question. Treat this as a topic source and a new post, not as a proven reach multiplier. Every reply still needs the first-3-seconds keyword and claim rules.

### 18. Reposting or remixing winners (Part 2 v2): any risk of being penalized

**Sources:**
1. [TikTok Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)

**Finding:** TikTok’s official rule is that content is ineligible for the For You feed if it includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new. The guideline does not say that reuploading your own video is an automatic ban or strike. The documented risk is For You ineligibility, not account removal. UNVERIFIED: that deleting and reuploading Part 2, or posting an unchanged Part 2 v2, will raise or reset its plays.

**Action for us:** Do not reupload the Part 2 cut, or any other winner, as the same file. If we make a Part 2 v2, it has to be a real new version (new hook, new on-screen claim, or new cut), or it can be kept off For You. Search already carried Part 2 (56.4%); a reused file could still be findable, but we should not risk the For You half (42.8% on that video) for a duplicate upload.

### 19. TikTok's AI-content labeling rules and their reach impact

**Sources:**
1. [TikTok Creator Academy: AI-generated content label](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/ai-generated-content-label) (last updated September 25, 2026)
2. [TikTok: AI-generated content](https://www.tiktok.com/tns-inapp/pages/ai-generated-content)

**Finding:** Official policy: adding the “Creator labeled as AI-generated” label “won’t affect the distribution of your video” if the video does not violate Community Guidelines (Creator Academy; the in-app AI-generated content page says the same). Creators are required to disclose AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking people or scenes. Unlabeled realistic AIGC may be removed, restricted, or labeled by TikTok. Small edits such as color correction, reframing, or cropping do not need the label. The Academy defines AI-generated content as any image, video, or audio made or changed by AI, including artistic styles such as anime, cartoons, or paintings, but the disclosure requirement it states is for realistic scenes, not for every cartoon. The label cannot be removed after posting. UNVERIFIED: that the label reduces plays, or that our cartoon style is required to carry it.

**Action for us:** Do not skip the AI label to chase reach; TikTok says the label does not change distribution. Apply it when the video is realistic AIGC or when we are required to disclose. Whether every cartoon Meatball video must be labeled is not settled by the pages in this pass, so treat a blanket “always on” or “never on” rule as unproven until Studio checks the in-app prompt.
