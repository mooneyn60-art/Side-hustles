## 1. fungus-gnats — Tue 11/17/2026 10:00 ET YouTube

Spoken script (58 words) plus hook card, trap card, and debate card.

### Hook / card

**Hook and line 1:** "Your house plant's female fungus gnats lay up to 200 eggs."  
**Card:** `YOUR HOUSE PLANT'S FEMALES` / `LAY UP TO 200 EGGS`  
**PASS.** CSU Extension (houseplant fact sheet): "During their seven to 10-day life span, females may lay up to 200 eggs into the cracks and crevices of growing media." UW Extension: "Fungus gnat females lay up to 200 eggs (in clusters) in cracks and crevices on the surface of the moist soil of potted plants." CSU PlantTalk uses a range, not a contradiction: "Adult females lay 75 to 200 eggs, which hatch in a week, in cracks in the potting mix surface." "Up to 200" and "female" are the right hedges. Naming house plants is fair. The older hook "Your plant soil hides up to 200 eggs" was correctly retired; the number belongs on the female, not the pot. The card should keep "fungus gnats" in the title or on the tag (the refit already notes this).

### Spoken fact lines

**Line 3:** "They hatch within a week."  
**PASS**, with an optional tighter hedge. PlantTalk: "which hatch in a week." UW: "The eggs typically hatch within three to six days." "Within a week" covers both. Optional FIX if the line is tightened: "They typically hatch within a week."

**Line 4:** "The maggots eat fungus and your plant's roots."  
**FIX.** The spoken line drops "organic matter in the potting mix" and states root-eating as routine "your plant's roots." PlantTalk: "The larvae, called maggots, feed on soil fungi, organic matter in the potting mix and on plant roots. Feeding on plant roots may injure houseplants or allow root-rotting fungi to enter." UW is more cautious: "Fungus gnat larvae typically feed on decaying organic matter and fungi in the soil. However, if larval numbers are particularly high, they may damage roots by feeding on root hairs or tunneling into the roots themselves. This sort of damage is rare in home settings." The refit's own hedge note says the root diet follows the full CSU sentence; the spoken line does not.

Corrected line: "The maggots feed on soil fungi, organic matter in the potting mix, and plant roots."  
If the cap is tight: "Maggots eat fungus and organic matter, and may feed on roots."

**PASS.** PlantTalk: "Yellow sticky traps suspended above houseplants may capture several adults." The spoken line keeps "may capture." "Several" and "suspended above houseplants" already belong in the description, as the refit says. Do not say the traps will wipe out the infestation. Trap card should keep "may."
