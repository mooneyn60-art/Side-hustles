### 22. Winter bug invaders: stink bugs, ladybugs, box elder bugs, cluster flies

University Extension sources treat brown marmorated stink bugs, multicolored Asian lady beetles, boxelder bugs, and cluster flies as the standard fall/winter home invaders: they gather on sunny walls and slip indoors to overwinter. They generally do not reproduce indoors. The usual problems are numbers, a foul odor or stains when crushed (stink bugs and Asian lady beetles), and nuisance around windows. [Modesto Bee / Extension roundup, updated 16 Nov 2026](https://www.modbee.com/living/article318100891.html); [University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/yard-and-garden-news/temperatures-fall-home-invading-insects-come-knocking); [Illinois Extension, Good Growing Ep. 227](https://extension.illinois.edu/podcasts/good-growing/ep-227-stink-bugs-lady-beetles-boxelder-invasions-falls-uninvited-houseguests)

Search-demand numbers from Softscotch (trailing 12-month averages through April 2026 — same vendor as knowledge-gaps, but these phrases were not logged there): “pest control for stink bugs” 49,500/month, peaks October; “pest control for box elder bugs” 22,200/month, peaks June. [Softscotch — 138 SEO Keywords for Pest Control Companies (2026 Data)](https://softscotch.com/seo-keywords-for-pest-control-companies/)

**UNVERIFIED:** monthly US search volumes for cluster flies, Asian lady beetles / “ladybugs in the house,” and for the question forms (“why are there stink bugs in my house”). The June peak on the box-elder *pest-control* phrase may not match the fall invasion window; treat that peak as the vendor’s figure, not as proof of when people ask ID questions.

### 25. Cockroaches: German vs. American, the common questions

Extension and pest-control explainers agree on the questions people ask: What’s the difference? How do I tell which one I have? Which is worse? Do they fly? Rutgers Cooperative Extension and the Illinois Department of Public Health both state the German cockroach is the most common species in US homes (kitchens and bathrooms; about ½ inch, two dark stripes, indoor-only, a generation in about 100 days). The American cockroach is much larger (1½–2 inches, reddish-brown), often called a palmetto bug or water bug, prefers basements, drains, and sewers, and can fly in warm conditions; Germans rarely fly. JC Ehrlich and EcoGuard frame the same comparison as size, stripes vs. yellow markings, indoor-only vs. indoor/outdoor, and reproduction speed. [Rutgers Cooperative Extension FS1327](https://extension.rutgers.edu/fs1327); [Illinois Department of Public Health — Cockroaches](https://dph.illinois.gov/topics-services/environmental-health-protection/structural-pest-control/cockroaches.html); [JC Ehrlich — German vs. American cockroaches](https://www.jcehrlich.com/pest-insights/cockroaches/american-vs-german-roaches); [EcoGuard — German vs American cockroaches](https://www.ecoguardpestmanagement.com/blog/cockroaches/german-vs-american-cockroaches)

### 29. Bug myths people believe (good for the "myth" angle in our name)

University entomology sources still spend time on the same few beliefs, which makes them usable on-camera myths.

The biggest one: "daddy long-legs are the most venomous spiders in the world, but their fangs are too small to bite." UF/IFAS (17 December 2025), UC Riverside Entomology, and Virginia Tech Extension all say this is false, and that "daddy long-legs" is used for three different animals: harvestmen (not spiders, no venom), cellar spiders (true spiders, medically insignificant venom), and crane flies (insects, no bite). [UF/IFAS Extension Manatee County](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/manateeco/2025/12/17/a-tale-of-three-daddy-long-legs/); [UCR Entomology](https://entomology.ucr.edu/news/2022/01/14/are-daddy-longlegs-really-most-venomous-spiders-world); [Virginia Tech ENTO-685](https://www.pubs.ext.vt.edu/content/pubs_ext_vt_edu/en/ENTO/ento-685-harvestmen.html)

Two others that are still in circulation: earwigs do not burrow into ears to lay eggs or eat brains (the name comes from that superstition); they can rarely walk into an ear and are then just removed. Cockroaches are more radiation-tolerant than humans (a cited lethal dose about 6 to 15 times that for humans) but are not uniquely nuclear-proof and would not survive the heat or blast of a bomb. [PubMed — Earwig Crawling in the Ear: Myth or Truth](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34094780/); [Weather Channel — 8 Myths About Spiders, Insects and Your Body](https://weather.com/health/news/myths-about-spiders-insects-and-your-body-20140916)

**UNVERIFIED:** search volumes for these myth phrases, and which myth is searched most.

### 30. Holiday and seasonal hooks: Christmas tree bugs, firewood bugs

A November 2026 survey of 1,004 US adults by Modern Pest found 22% have found pests in their Christmas trees, while 39% had never considered that a tree could harbor pests. Of those who found pests, the most common were spiders (46%), ants (21%), and aphids (9%). More than half inspect the tree (58%) or have it shaken (50%); 33% let it sit in the garage. 28% spray pesticide on the tree; the same article says not to use aerosol insect sprays on trees because many are flammable. [Modern Pest — Holiday Pest Control Statistics](https://www.modernpest.com/blog/holiday-pest-control-statistics/)

A November 2026 pest-control explainer lists the usual tree hitchhikers (aphids, bark beetles, mites, praying-mantis egg cases, psocids, scale, spiders) and two firewood groups: insects already in the wood (termites, carpenter ants, wood-boring beetles, wasps) and animals using the pile for shelter (earwigs, centipedes, millipedes, cockroaches, Asian lady beetles, scorpions, black widows). Advice: shake the tree outdoors, bring in only the firewood you will burn that day. [Northwest Exterminating — Christmas Tree Insects and Other Unwanted Holiday Pests](https://www.callnorthwest.com/2026/11/unwanted-christmas-pest-presents/)

No monthly volume for "Christmas tree bugs" or "firewood bugs" was found. "Christmas tree" itself is estimated at about 2.1 million US searches in each of November and December 2025, which is the tree, not the insects. [KWS Trends](https://www.kwstrends.com/blog/monthly-search-average-hides-seasonal-peaks)

**UNVERIFIED:** search volumes for "Christmas tree bugs," "bugs in Christmas tree," or "firewood bugs."
