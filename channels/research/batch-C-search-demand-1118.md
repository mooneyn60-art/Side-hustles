### 21. The top-searched house bugs in the US, by season (Nov–Feb)

A 2026 US pest-control PPC dataset shows overall “pest control” search demand is lower in November–February than in spring and summer, but it does not fall off: Nov 2025 1,198,700; Dec 2025 1,224,820; Jan 2026 1,331,500; Feb 2026 1,182,500, versus a May 2026 peak of 1,854,700. On the same page, the highest named-pest keywords are “bed bug treatment” (201,000/month) and “cockroach exterminator” (165,000/month), even with or just behind generic “pest control” / “pest control near me.” That keyword table is not broken out by month, so it is not a proven Nov–Feb species ranking. [MB Adv — Pest Control PPC Statistics 2026](https://www.mbadv.agency/ppc-industry-statistics/pest-control-ppc-statistics)

A separate pest-keyword CSV shows “bed bugs bite” still large in winter, though below its late-summer peak: 550,000 in Dec 2025 and Jan 2026, and 450,000 in Feb 2026, versus 1,220,000 in Sep 2025 and Jun–Aug 2026. These vendor figures are much higher than MB Adv’s “bed bug treatment” number; treat them as relative seasonality, not as a single official volume. [DataForTrades pest-control keywords sample](https://datafortrades.com/samples/pest-control-research/keywords.csv)

### 22. Winter bug invaders: stink bugs, ladybugs, box elder bugs, cluster flies

University Extension sources treat brown marmorated stink bugs, multicolored Asian lady beetles, boxelder bugs, and cluster flies as the standard fall/winter home invaders: they gather on sunny walls and slip indoors to overwinter. They generally do not reproduce indoors. The usual problems are numbers, a foul odor or stains when crushed (stink bugs and Asian lady beetles), and nuisance around windows. [Modesto Bee / Extension roundup, updated 16 Nov 2026](https://www.modbee.com/living/article318100891.html); [University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/yard-and-garden-news/temperatures-fall-home-invading-insects-come-knocking); [Illinois Extension, Good Growing Ep. 227](https://extension.illinois.edu/podcasts/good-growing/ep-227-stink-bugs-lady-beetles-boxelder-invasions-falls-uninvited-houseguests)

Search-demand numbers from Softscotch (trailing 12-month averages through April 2026 — same vendor as knowledge-gaps, but these phrases were not logged there): “pest control for stink bugs” 49,500/month, peaks October; “pest control for box elder bugs” 22,200/month, peaks June. [Softscotch — 138 SEO Keywords for Pest Control Companies (2026 Data)](https://softscotch.com/seo-keywords-for-pest-control-companies/)

**UNVERIFIED:** monthly US search volumes for cluster flies, Asian lady beetles / “ladybugs in the house,” and for the question forms (“why are there stink bugs in my house”). The June peak on the box-elder *pest-control* phrase may not match the fall invasion window; treat that peak as the vendor’s figure, not as proof of when people ask ID questions.

### 24. Bed bugs: the most common questions

A 2025–2026 pest-keyword sample dataset puts bite and presence queries far above treatment queries. The largest listed phrases are “bed bugs bite” and “bed bugs in bed,” at 823,000 average monthly searches each. “Bed bugs bite” is seasonal: about 1,220,000 in Sep 2025 and Jun–Aug 2026, still 550,000 in Dec 2025 and Jan 2026, and 450,000 in Feb 2026 (the low month). Other question-like phrases in the same file: “bed bugs what do bites look like” 90,500/month; “how can you get rid of bed bugs” 90,500/month. Hire or treatment phrases are smaller: “treating for bed bugs” 201,000; “exterminating bed bugs” 135,000. That supports ID-and-bite questions as the main search demand, including in winter, not only “how to kill bed bugs” (a phrase knowledge-gaps already noted without a volume). ### 25. Cockroaches: German vs. American, the common questions

Extension and pest-control explainers agree on the questions people ask: What’s the difference? How do I tell which one I have? Which is worse? Do they fly? Rutgers Cooperative Extension and the Illinois Department of Public Health both state the German cockroach is the most common species in US homes (kitchens and bathrooms; about ½ inch, two dark stripes, indoor-only, a generation in about 100 days). The American cockroach is much larger (1½–2 inches, reddish-brown), often called a palmetto bug or water bug, prefers basements, drains, and sewers, and can fly in warm conditions; Germans rarely fly. JC Ehrlich and EcoGuard frame the same comparison as size, stripes vs. yellow markings, indoor-only vs. indoor/outdoor, and reproduction speed. [Rutgers Cooperative Extension FS1327](https://extension.rutgers.edu/fs1327); [Illinois Department of Public Health — Cockroaches](https://dph.illinois.gov/topics-services/environmental-health-protection/structural-pest-control/cockroaches.html); [JC Ehrlich — German vs. American cockroaches](https://www.jcehrlich.com/pest-insights/cockroaches/american-vs-german-roaches); [EcoGuard — German vs American cockroaches](https://www.ecoguardpestmanagement.com/blog/cockroaches/german-vs-american-cockroaches)
