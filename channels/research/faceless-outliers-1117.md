### 1b. The pattern, and where we differ

- **One question per video, about a bug the viewer already has at home.** Zyka and Stone break out on objects in the viewer's own house (the spider you threw out, the centipede in the bathroom). They do not use exotic animals. [FETCHED + OUR ANALYSIS] Our current plan centers on pet-POV stories, so a house-bug question episode is a separate lane we don't run as a long-form today.
- **Long-form first, often with no Shorts at all.** Zyka (10.7K subs in about 6 months) and Stone (23.6K) have **0 Shorts**. All of their watch hours come from long-form, which is the only kind that counts toward YPP. [FETCHED] We post 3 Shorts a day. Our 28-day Shorts views were 85.3K, but qualified hours sit at 12 (`studio-read-1117.md`).
- **Short isn't fatal.** Zyka's 3–4.5 min videos still pulled millions of views. Length follows the question; Zyka does not pad to 8–10 min. [FETCHED] [UNCERTAIN] We can't see their average view duration, so we can't say which length earns more hours.
- **Calm narrator voice with a sympathetic angle on the bug.** Titles ask, and the answer comes in the video. This matches the "ask, don't answer" title rule already in TASKS (11/16 5:20 PM note, item 4). [FETCHED titles and descriptions]
- **Fast follow-up after an outlier.** Zyka posted a same-subject sequel 9 days after the 2.47M video, and the sequel got 584K. Stone posted one sequel on a new bug 20 days later and got 87K, then stopped. [FETCHED]
- **Lean-back runtime for TV hours.** Bound compiles its own episodes into 2-hour videos, and Sleepless Historian makes 2-hour originals. vidIQ: "For a 2-hour video, that number shrinks to just 2,000 views to hit 4,000 hours" [VENDOR CLAIM; this assumes full watch-through]: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FltNsyPXNdo (transcript mirror, not YouTube's: https://pickscribe.com/v/FltNsyPXNdo).
- **vidIQ's thesis:** "find a format that works … then mold it into your own voice and tone and niche … the discipline to repeat this process again and again." It also says to borrow authority from recognizable subjects, with "no fake quotes … no fabricated drama." [VENDOR CLAIM] Same source as above. vidIQ's earnings figures are its own tool estimates and are not cited here.

**The common thread [OUR ANALYSIS]:** in every documented case the hours came from **one long-form outlier**. None came from Shorts. Subs were met before hours (the same position we're in: 1,019 subs, 12 hours). Each creator **made a follow-up in the same format right away**, and the follow-up drew far less than the outlier (28K vs 220K; Zyka's 584K vs 2.47M). In Kamillah Rae's case, revenue plateaued after the spike.

**Rough math [OUR ARITHMETIC, assumed AVDs; we have no long-form AVD data, per `studio-read-1117.md`]:** we need 4,000 − 12 = 3,988 hours. That is about **60K views at a 4:00 average view duration**, or about **120K views at 2:00**. A single Zyka-scale hit would cover it. A 2-hour compilation averaging 20 minutes watched would need about 12K views.

## 3. What to copy vs not, against official policy

### 3a. The policy, quoted [OFFICIAL]

From the YouTube channel monetization policies, Help 1311392 (fetched 11/17): https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392

- **Rename (July 15, 2025):** "We're making a minor update to our 'repetitious content' policy to better clarify this includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced. We are also renaming this policy from 'repetitious content' to 'inauthentic content.' … There is no change to our reused content policy…" The current Help section is titled "Generic or Repetitive Content."
- **Allowed:** "Similar content, like a series following a set of characters across episodes … but in which each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept."
- **Not allowed:**
  - "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome (i.e., using a highly similar storyline template across multiple videos)"
  - "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"
  - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective"
- **Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content, not allowed:**
  - "Channels that heavily rely on generic templates or emotionally manipulative themes, like a series showing repetitive scenarios of animals in exaggerated distress or peril"
  - "Content that repeatedly uses disturbing themes (such as violence or loss) without building a cohesive narrative"
- **Allowed in the same section:** "Content that expresses your unique creative voice, like using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented."
- **Reused content:** "channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value." "This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator."
- **What reviewers look at:** "Main theme, Most viewed videos, Newest videos, Biggest proportion of watch time, Video metadata…, Channel's 'About' section."
- **YouTube Community staff post (July 2025):** https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251/response-to-creator-questions-about-ypp-policies-july-2025?hl=en-GB
  - Its mass-produced examples include "A channel that uploads narrated stories with only superficial differences between them" and "A channel that uploads slideshows that all have the same narration."
  - "These guidelines apply regardless of how the content was made."
  - Rene Ritchie's Mythbuster says the same: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uM4M8BzrT4E

### 3b. Copy / don't copy

| Copy | Don't copy |
|---|---|
| Zyka's **one household-bug question per episode**, with a distinct fact payload each time (this fits "distinct storyline, focus, or concept") | **Word-for-word title clones.** NEXEAT got 269 views against 584K, and Myrk got 4.5K against 2.47M. This also risks reused-content review. Stone already owns "Why It Sucks to be Born as a House Centipede," so don't reuse that title |
| A **same-format follow-up within about 7–10 days** of any outlier (Zyka, Sykora) | **One outlier, then silence.** Stone stopped after 3 videos |
| **Format lineage with credit and our own voice.** Bound openly says it was "Inspired by Dinzo." vidIQ: "mold it into your own voice" | **The same peril, ending or template every episode.** This maps onto the "same situation … same outcome" and "animals in exaggerated distress or peril" bullets. The 11/16 guardrail (TASKS item 5: peril in at most 1 in 3 episodes, WMS template retired as a daily series) is the right defense. Keep it |
| **Long lean-back runtime** built from our own *long-form* episodes, with new linking narration (Bound's pattern) | Compilations stitched from Shorts. That breaks the long-form rule and reads as low-effort. [UNCERTAIN] Whether frequent self-compilations attract "repetitive" review is not addressed in Help. Keep them occasional |
| Lead with educational substance (sources, real biology) | "AI slop" signals: still images, identical narration beats, or a "Meatball lived"-style stock ending that repeats across episodes |

**Policy risk [OUR ANALYSIS]:** reviewers look at the "Most viewed videos" and the "Biggest proportion of watch time." If our top videos are near-identical WMS peril Shorts, those videos define the channel at review time, even if the long-forms are varied. Mixing in house-bug question long-forms and varying the pet-POV outcomes lowers that risk. [UNCERTAIN] YouTube publishes no threshold for "repetitive."

## 4. Five concrete changes for Meatball (proposals for Social Studio; none overrides the 11/16 order)

1. **Make one of the 3 weekly long-forms a house-bug question episode, about 4–6 min.** Use the existing TASKS pattern "What Happens If You [do X] to a [house bug]?" with our own wording, not Zyka's titles. Why: we have **12 of 4,000** qualified hours, and the only documented route to closing that gap is a long-form outlier. Zyka's 3:26 house-bug question hit 2.47M views with 0 Shorts.
2. **Add an outlier trigger.** If any long-form passes 10× our long-form median views in 7 days, the Showrunner slots a same-format sequel on a different bug or scenario within 7–10 days, with an end screen and a pinned comment to the original. Why: in every documented case the hours came from one hit and the creator followed up fast (Zyka 9 days, Sykora "make another"). **3,988 hours is about 60K views at a 4:00 AVD** [assumed AVD], which is a single outlier.
3. **Once 6 or more distinct pet-POV or bug long-forms exist, propose one 60–120 min "full season" compilation** of our own long-forms, with new linking narration. Originality QA signs off first, and Moondog88 approves. Why: Bound and Sleepless Historian bank TV lean-back hours this way, and at a 20-minute AVD, about 12K views would cover the gap. Subs are already done (**1,019**), so hours are the only blocker before the 1/31/2027 deadline.
4. **Add an Originality QA checklist line tied to Help 1311392.** Each episode needs a distinct outcome (not the same "nearly dies, then lives" ending), a distinct fact payload, and no exaggerated-distress framing. Run a channel-level check of our top-10 most viewed and top-watch-time videos before any YPP application. Why: reviewers judge "Most viewed videos" and "Biggest proportion of watch time," and the policy names "repetitive scenarios of animals in exaggerated distress or peril."
5. **Judge Shorts and TikTok on their own goals, not as YouTube hour feeders.** TikTok is at **9,404** followers, 596 short of the 10K Rewards bar (`swot-1117.md`). Keep TikTok chapters aimed at that bar. On YouTube, when Shorts and long-form compete for render time, long-form wins. Why: Shorts-feed hours don't count, Zyka and Stone grew with 0 Shorts, and our 85.3K Shorts views in 28 days left qualified hours at 12.
