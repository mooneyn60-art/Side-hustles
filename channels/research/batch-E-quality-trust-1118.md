## Part 1 (topics 41-45)

### 41. TTS voice perception: do AI voices hurt retention? Any studies.

**Finding:** There is no large, independent YouTube-platform study that isolates TTS versus human narration on Shorts or long-form retention. The closest peer-reviewed experiment, on educational short videos for language learning, found that an AI voice did not hurt engagement and that engagement rose and extraneous cognitive load fell only when both the voice and the on-screen avatar were AI-generated. [Springer — Education and Information Technologies, "AI versus human-generated voices and avatars" (2025)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-025-13654-x)

A 2026 faceless-YouTube creator test (96 videos, same scripts and edits, four voices including ElevenLabs and a professional human narrator) is not peer-reviewed, but it is the most detailed YouTube retention comparison found in this pass. At 30 seconds, the best AI voices matched or beat the human narrator in two of three niches (for example, space/science: ElevenLabs 68% still watching vs human 66%). By 5 minutes the human lead was 4 points in that niche (43% vs 39%), and by 15 minutes it was 6 points (24% vs 18%). The authors say the gap opens after about 4 minutes of continuous narration, when the voice starts to feel flat. [Vaatik — AI Voice vs Human Voice YouTube Retention (2026 Test)](https://vaatik.com/blog/youtube-faceless-voice-ai-vs-humano-retencion)

**Action for us:** Do not treat "AI voices hurt retention" as a reason to drop voice E2. For Shorts (under ~2 minutes), the sourced YouTube test shows little or no penalty at 30 seconds, and the academic short-video study does not show a penalty. For Wednesday long-form, which we already plan to run 8-12 minutes, the same creator test predicts a mid-video retention gap unless the narration changes energy. Keep E2, and treat mid-video music, visual, or delivery shifts on long-form as the place to watch, not a voice swap. UNVERIFIED: that E2 itself underperforms a human read on our channel; we have not A/B tested it.

### 42. Caption style (word-by-word vs. phrase) and retention.

**Finding:** No independent controlled study isolates word-by-word captions against phrase captions for YouTube or TikTok retention. One caption-tool vendor reports an A/B test of 40 Shorts in which word-by-word captions averaged 31.7 seconds viewed and 79% watched, versus 18.2 seconds and 48% for sentence-level captions. That result is not independently verified and should not be treated as a fact. [VidNo — Word-by-Word Captions for Shorts](https://vidno.ai/blog/word-by-word-caption-generator-shorts) UNVERIFIED as a general rule.

A 2026 eye-tracking study of 211 people watching a vertical video found that two-line subtitles drew more visual attention than one-line subtitles (longer total fixation, more revisits, lower skip rate), and the difference remained after controlling for character count. That study is about line count, not highlight style, but it supports giving viewers enough on-screen context to read, not a single flashing word. [Applied Cognitive Psychology — "Attention Allocation During Viewing of One-Line and Two-Line Subtitles in Vertical Videos"](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.70262)

Creator-tool writeups that do not claim a controlled test recommend isolated one-to-three-word flashes for stories and hooks, and phrase captions with the current word highlighted for explanations, facts, numbers, and names, because viewers can read slightly ahead. [AutoAIShorts — Caption Styles That Improve Retention](https://www.autoaishorts.com/blog/caption-styles-that-retain); [Blitzcut — Word-by-Word Captions vs Full-Sentence Captions in 2026](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/word-by-word-captions-vs-full-sentence-captions) (Blitzcut states it has no controlled test).

**Action for us:** Keep captions on from the first spoken word (already required so the bug name is shown in the first 3 seconds). Default to a short phrase on screen with the spoken word highlighted, not isolated one-word flashes, so scientific names, bite counts, and "is this a brown recluse?" style questions stay readable. UNVERIFIED: that switching our current caption style would lift retention on Meatball videos.

### 43. Pacing: cuts per minute in top animated Shorts.

**Finding:** No sourced study in this pass measured animated educational Shorts only. The closest measured set is a frame-by-frame pacing study of 54 viral YouTube Shorts, published October 15, 2026. Median shot length was 4.7 seconds (about 13 cuts or reframes per minute). The fastest quarter averaged 2.8 seconds per shot. The first cut typically landed at 1.9 seconds. 47 of 54 videos (87%) had at least one cut. The authors note the sample was chosen for performance, not at random, and that a tighter crop of the same shot counts as a cut for the viewer; slow zooms were not counted. [Viibeo — Viral Shorts Pacing Study: Shot Length in 54 Videos](https://www.viibeo.com/research/viral-shorts-pacing)

A separate breakdown of 22 high-performing videos (not Shorts-only, not animation-only) found something on screen changed every 3 to 8 seconds, with a median around 5 seconds, and long-form visual-change rates of about 7 to 13 per minute. [PandaStudio — The 7 laws of retention editing, from 22 top videos](https://www.writepanda.ai/blog/retention-editing-7-laws/)

**Action for us:** In Shorts, change the picture within the first 2 seconds, and cut, punch in, or swap a graphic every 3-5 seconds so a static cartoon hold does not run past the ~4.7-second median. Slow slightly (about 2-4 seconds) on ID and fact cards so the name can be read. UNVERIFIED: that our current v4/v5 cuts already sit in this range; Studio should measure a recent Short before treating this as a fix.

### 44. Sound design: does SFX density affect retention?

**Finding:** No sourced study isolates sound-effect count, or "SFX density," against YouTube or Shorts retention. Claims that a specific number of effects per minute lifts retention are UNVERIFIED.

The closest academic result is a 2026 computational study of 1,200 short videos, later checked on 14,492 more, using message sensation value (loudness, tempo, and brightness — not a count of sound effects). Sensory engagement rose with higher sensation value, but behavioral engagement followed an inverted U: moderate intensity did best, and stacking more intensity did not keep raising it. [arXiv — "A Computational Model of Message Sensation Value in Short Video Multimodal Features that Predicts Sensory and Behavioral Engagement"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19995)

Practical editing guidance, not a controlled test, says each effect should mark a transition, an on-screen emphasis, or a payoff, and that a whoosh on every cut becomes noise. [Asendify playbook — Does Sound Design Improve YouTube Audience Retention?](https://playbook.asendify.co/blog/does-sound-design-improve-youtube-audience-retention)

**Action for us:** Do not add more whooshes or hits just to raise "density." Use SFX to mark a cut, a fact card, or the reveal, and keep the voice in front. UNVERIFIED: that our current SFX mix is too thin or too thick.

### 45. YouTube policy on reused or repetitive content (the risk of cutting Shorts from the long-form).

**Finding:** YouTube's official Studio documentation says creators can cut clips and Shorts from their own long-form videos and that "You won't be penalized for using your content in a video clip or Short." [YouTube Help — Video Clips & Shorts in YouTube Studio](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15824265) YouTube also ships a native "edit into a Short" path for a public long-form video the creator uploaded, and those Shorts can link back to the original. [YouTube Help — Create YouTube Shorts from your videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12836917)

The reused-content monetization policy is aimed at channels that repurpose other people's content from YouTube or elsewhere without significant original commentary, substantive edits, or educational or entertainment value. It is applied to the channel as a whole and is separate from copyright. Examples of monetization-ineligible Shorts include unedited clips from other people's movies or TV, reuploads of other creators' work, and compilations with no original content. [YouTube Help — Channel monetization policies (reused content)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [YouTube Help — Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)

On July 15, 2025, YouTube said it was renaming "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" and clarifying that the policy includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced. That update is about inauthentic or mass-produced material, not about posting a Short cut from your own original long-form. [YouTube Help — Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)

**Action for us:** Keep cutting Shorts from our own long-form. Official policy does not penalize that. The remaining risk is weaker and partly already covered in Batch B: a Short that is only a bare recap, or a mass of near-identical cuts, is a weaker video and could look repetitive. Each Short should still work on its own, open with the search question and the bug's name, and use a Related-video link back to the long-form. UNVERIFIED: that our current Shorts-from-long-form workflow would fail a reused-content or inauthentic-content review.

## Supported changes (part 1)

1. **In every Short, change the picture within the first 2 seconds, and cut, punch in, or swap a graphic every 3-5 seconds.** Hold ID and fact cards about 2-4 seconds so the name can be read. Supported by the Viibeo frame-by-frame study of 54 viral Shorts (median shot 4.7 seconds, first cut about 1.9 seconds), published October 15, 2026. [Viibeo](https://www.viibeo.com/research/viral-shorts-pacing). UNVERIFIED until Studio measures our current cut rate: whether this is already true of the v4/v5 Shorts.

2. **Keep cutting Shorts from our own Wednesday and Saturday long-forms.** YouTube says you will not be penalized for using your own content in a clip or Short, and it provides a tool to do it. Still make each Short stand alone (unique hook and title, bug name in the first 3 seconds, Related-video link), so it is not a bare recap. Supported by [YouTube Help — Video Clips & Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15824265) and [YouTube Help — Create Shorts from your videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12836917). Reused-content rules target other people's material, not our own originals. [YouTube Help — Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).

3. **Do not replace voice E2, and do not add SFX just to raise density.** For Shorts, the sourced TTS test shows little or no retention gap at 30 seconds, and a peer-reviewed educational short-video study did not find an AI-voice penalty. For 8-12 minute long-form, watch mid-video retention and add energy shifts (music, visual change, or delivery) rather than a voice swap. Use sound effects only to mark a transition, a fact card, or a reveal. Supported by [Springer, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-025-13654-x); [Vaatik 2026 test](https://vaatik.com/blog/youtube-faceless-voice-ai-vs-humano-retencion) (creator test, not peer-reviewed); [arXiv MSV study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19995) (inverted-U for audio intensity; SFX count itself was not measured).
