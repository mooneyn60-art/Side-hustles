# Batch A: YouTube growth mechanics (topics 1–10)

Social Studio research, 2026-11-18 (America/New_York).
Channel context used in this pass: about 1,010 subscribers; 12.7 of 4,000 long-form watch hours; long-form CTR 4.4–5.4% with only about 1,400 impressions per video; a Wednesday ~7-minute ranking long-form and a Saturday 4-minute question long-form; 3 Shorts a day.

Rules: every claim has a source link. Claims without a source are marked UNVERIFIED. Topics 1 and 3 are brief because they are also covered in the parallel knowledge-gaps pass; most of this file is on topics 2 and 4–10.

## 1. Shorts frequency: does 3 a day help or cannibalize?

**Sources (1, brief):** [ReelsBot, “How often should you upload?”](https://reelsbot.co/blog/does-posting-more-grow-a-channel) — 164 channels, Shorts stretches from January 2025 to October 2026.

**Finding:** In that sample, posting more Shorts did not lower views per Short. Stretches at 2+ Shorts a day had the same median views per Short as quieter stretches (100% of the channel’s typical stretch). A regression on 1,017 stretches since 2025 estimated +0.05 ± 0.05 per doubling of cadence: no detectable per-Short penalty, and no bonus either. This does not measure subscriber growth, audience fatigue, or quality drop if the extra Shorts get weaker. UNVERIFIED: that 3 a day is the best cadence for this channel, or that it helps or hurts long-form.

**Action for us:** Keep the 3-Shorts-a-day volume if quality stays even with the flagship look. Judge it on our own views-per-Short and long-form hours on the Monday scorecard, not on a universal “3 is too many” rule.

## 2. Shorts to long-form conversion; how the Related video link performs

**Sources:**
1. [YouTube Help: Add a related video to your YouTube Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)
2. [YouTube Official Blog, 14 Jul 2026: “How to convert YouTube Shorts views into long-form channel growth”](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
3. [YouTube Help: Understand your YouTube video reach](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?hl=en) (Shorts is a tracked traffic source on long-form)

**Finding:** YouTube’s Related video feature puts a clickable link under the channel handle in the Shorts player, pointing to a public or unlisted Video, Short, or Live on the same channel. It requires advanced feature access. YouTube’s July 2026 creator guide says the link alone is not enough: match the linked long-form to the Short’s topic; add a spoken and on-screen call to action in the last 5 seconds of the Short (including pointing down at the link or flashing the long-form thumbnail); and start the long-form in the first 5–10 seconds on the exact question or hook the Short promised, with no channel-logo intro. The same guide says some Shorts viewers watch Shorts only and may never click a 10-minute video, so conversion has to be deliberate. Studio’s Reach tab reports traffic from “Shorts” separately, which is how we should measure the link. YouTube does not publish a typical click-through or conversion rate. UNVERIFIED: any specific conversion percentage for Related video.

**Action for us:** Put a Related video link on every Short, pointing to the same-bug long-form (not a random latest upload). End each Short with a spoken “full version is in the link below” plus a downward point or thumbnail flash. Open the Wednesday and Saturday long-forms on the bug question, not a logo. After each drop, check Reach → traffic source “Shorts” on that long-form.

## 3. How Test & Compare picks its winner, and how long it runs

**Sources (1, brief):** [YouTube Help: A/B test titles & thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)

**Finding:** The test compares up to 3 titles, thumbnails, or title-and-thumbnail pairs. The winner is the option with the highest watch-time share, not the highest click-through rate. YouTube says it optimizes for watch time because a title and thumbnail should also set the right expectation. Tests usually finish in a few days, and at most in two weeks. Results are Winner (statistically significant watch-time lead), Performed Same, or Inconclusive. If there is no clear winner, the first option uploaded becomes the default. YouTube warns that similar variants take longer, and that too few impressions often produce Inconclusive rather than Winner. Tests are not available on Shorts, Made for Kids, private, or premiere videos (a premiere can be tested only after it becomes a normal long-form).

**Action for us:** Run tonight’s 3-thumbnail test, but make the three designs clearly different (not three crops of the same frame). With only about 1,400 impressions per video, Inconclusive is likely; if that happens, pick manually and re-test later or on an older video with more impressions, as YouTube itself suggests starting with older videos.

## 4. Browse vs. search vs. suggested traffic for small channels (under 5K subs)

**Sources:**
1. [YouTube Help: Understand your YouTube video reach](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?hl=en)
2. [YouTube Help: YouTube’s Recommendation System](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387?hl=en)
3. [YouTube Help: Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)

**Finding:** YouTube splits discovery into named sources. Browse features is Home, Subscriptions, Watch Later, and Trending/Explore. YouTube search is people typing a query. Suggested videos are recommendations next to or after other videos, plus links in descriptions. Shorts, End screens, and Playlists are counted separately. Recommendations are personalized from watch history and “interest affinity,” and from whether people click, watch, and positively engage when a video is shown. YouTube also tells creators to use searchable titles for people already looking for the topic, and to judge packaging by Home and Suggested CTR in the first 24 hours on videos with above-average impressions. YouTube does not publish a typical Browse/Search/Suggested mix for channels under 5,000 subscribers. UNVERIFIED: third-party claims that small channels get a fixed 40–60% of views from Search. What we can say from our own numbers: CTR of 4.4–5.4% is in a usable range, but about 1,400 impressions means the video is barely being shown on Browse and Suggested, so the bottleneck is impressions, not clicks.

**Action for us:** Write long-form titles and descriptions as the questions people type (“Do house centipedes bite?”, “Are cellar spiders dangerous?”), and check each video’s Reach tab for Search terms, Browse, Suggested, and Shorts. Do not treat a “good CTR” as success while impressions stay around 1,400.

## 5. Long-form length sweet spot for watch hours on small channels

**Sources:**
1. [SociaVault, 8 Nov 2026: “How Long Should a YouTube Video Be?”](https://sociavault.com/blog/how-long-should-a-youtube-video-be) — 2,010 long-form videos on 80 channels, pulled October 2026
2. [YouTube Help: Partner Program overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en) (only public long-form watch hours count toward the 4,000-hour path)
3. [YouTube Official Blog, 12 Aug 2026: qualified watch hours and Shorts views](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-monetization-qualified-watch-hours-shorts-views/)

**Finding:** In SociaVault’s same-channel comparison, videos under 8 minutes underperformed the channel’s usual views (0.75× under 4 minutes; 0.92× at 4–8 minutes). The 12–40 minute band was the flat sweet spot (1.00× to 1.02×). Education’s sample median was 101 minutes (pulled up by long documentaries); pets was 10.5 minutes and home was 24 minutes, closer to our niche. After YouTube’s 27 August 2026 change that counts a view as soon as a video starts playing, the view-count gap between short and long videos narrowed; watch time still rewards length. For YPP, only public long-form (and archived livestream) watch hours count; Shorts hours do not. A 4-minute Saturday video, even if fully watched, can add at most 4 minutes per view; a 10-minute video watched halfway adds 5 minutes. Our Wednesday ~7-minute flagship sits just under the 8-minute line that lagged on views; the Saturday 4-minute video sits in the weakest view band in that study. UNVERIFIED: that stretching our current scripts past 12 minutes would raise hours if retention fell sharply.

**Action for us:** Keep the Wednesday flagship at about 8–12 minutes of real content, not padded runtime. Treat the Saturday 4-minute slot as the weak one for watch hours: if a question episode can honestly hold 8–10 minutes, let it. Never add filler just to cross a minute mark.

## 6. Packaging: what title and thumbnail patterns lift CTR in education/animation

**Sources:**
1. [YouTube Help: Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)
2. [YouTube Help: A/B test titles & thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)
3. [YouTube Help: YouTube’s Recommendation System](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387?hl=en)

**Finding:** YouTube says 90% of the best-performing videos have custom thumbnails. Titles should be accurate (a mismatch makes people leave, which can hurt discovery), short, with the important words first, and with episode numbers and branding at the end. Limit ALL CAPS and emoji. YouTube distinguishes searchable titles (clearly state what the video is) from intriguing titles (curiosity for people not already searching). Thumbnails should be simple, high-resolution, readable on small screens, and can use the rule of thirds plus a little on-thumbnail text. A consistent title and thumbnail style helps viewers recognize the channel. YouTube judges a test winner by watch time, not CTR, and tells creators to measure Home/Suggested CTR in the first 24 hours. There is no official, education-or-animation-specific CTR lift number. UNVERIFIED: any claim that a particular character count, face, or color raises CTR by a set percentage.

**Action for us:** Lead titles with the bug and the question (“House centipedes: do they bite?”), and put “Meatball” or a part number at the end if at all. Keep custom thumbnails simple: one clear bug, one short line of text, high contrast. Use Test & Compare to pick on watch time, and read Home/Suggested CTR only on videos that actually get impressions.

## 7. End screens and playlists: their measured impact on session time

**Sources:**
1. [YouTube Help: Add end screens to videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789?hl=en)
2. [YouTube Help: YouTube’s Recommendation System](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387?hl=en)
3. [YouTube Help: Understand your YouTube video reach](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?hl=en)

**Finding:** End screens go in the last 5–20 seconds of a video at least 25 seconds long, with up to four elements on a 16:9 video (video, playlist, Subscribe, or another channel; external links only after YPP). YouTube’s own advice is to feature something relevant, use a spoken call to action, and leave time and space in the last 20 seconds. End screens are not shown on Made for Kids videos, in YouTube Music or YouTube Kids, or on most mobile web, and viewers can hide them. YouTube’s recommendation article tells creators to use playlists, end screens, and clear “if you liked this, watch…” prompts to help people find more of the channel, and says positive viewer interactions are a strong recommendation signal. Studio tracks End screens and Playlists as their own traffic sources, and reports “End screen element click rate” on the Engagement tab. YouTube does not publish a typical click rate or a session-time lift. UNVERIFIED: any specific percentage that end screens or playlists raise session time or watch hours.

**Action for us:** Put an end screen on every long-form: one element for the other weekly long-form or “best for viewer,” one for a House Guests playlist, and Subscribe if there is room. Leave a clean 15–20 seconds at the end. Keep videos not made for kids or the end screens will not show. After each upload, record end-screen click rate and playlist traffic in the daily Studio numbers.

## 8. Pinned comments and community posts: whether they help reach

**Sources:**
1. [YouTube Help: Start your Community](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15739414?hl=en)
2. [YouTube Help: YouTube’s Recommendation System](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387?hl=en)
3. [YouTube Help: Understand your YouTube video reach](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?hl=en) (Notifications and Channel pages are tracked; pinned comments are not a traffic source)

**Finding:** Community posts “may be recommended to viewers on their homepage, Subscriptions, and Watch Next feed.” The Community feature requires existing access to posts, a channel not set as Made for Kids, and an owner over 16. YouTube’s recommendation system counts comments, likes, and shares among the engagement signals it learns from, but it does not say that pinning a comment on a video increases that video’s impressions or recommendations. Pinned comments are also not listed as a traffic source. UNVERIFIED: that pinning a comment raises a video’s reach or views. Notifications to subscribers who have the bell on are a real, measured traffic source, but that is not the same as a pin or a Community post.

**Action for us:** Keep pinning a useful comment (the question the video answers, a link to the related long-form, or the next episode) because it helps the people who already opened the video. Use a Community post to announce each weekly long-form, since those posts can appear on Home and Subscriptions. Do not count pins as a growth lever until our own numbers show otherwise.

## 9. Upload time and day for long-form, US audience

**Sources:**
1. [YouTube Help: YouTube’s Recommendation System](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387?hl=en)
2. [Buffer, 24 Jul 2026: “Best Time to Post on YouTube,” 1.8 million videos](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
3. [SociaVault, 8 Nov 2026 length-and-day study](https://sociavault.com/blog/how-long-should-a-youtube-video-be)

**Finding:** YouTube’s official recommendation article is clear: “publish time is not known to impact a video’s long-term performance.” Posting when the audience is most active “might lead to more immediate views,” and YouTube only specifically advises using audience-active times for Premieres and livestreams. Buffer’s July 2026 analysis of 1.8 million videos found Sunday at 10 a.m. was the single strongest slot for long-form, with Sunday, Tuesday, and Monday the strongest days; Shorts peaked Friday at 4 p.m., 6 p.m., and 7 p.m. Buffer’s article, as reviewed, does not clearly state the timezone for those clock times. SociaVault, using New York time, found Saturday only slightly above a channel’s usual views (1.06×) and every block from 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. Eastern within 2% of normal. The two studies do not agree on a must-use day, which fits YouTube’s “don’t overthink publish time” guidance. Buffer also tells creators to check Studio → Analytics → Audience → “When your viewers are on YouTube” (last 28 days).

**Action for us:** Keep the Wednesday and Saturday long-form slots unless our own “When your viewers are on YouTube” report shows a much stronger window. Do not move a video just to chase a generic “best time” list. If we premiere or go live, schedule that for our audience’s peak hours.

## 10. YPP: current rules, and whether Shorts views count toward the 10M path

**Sources:**
1. [YouTube Help: Partner Program overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
2. [YouTube Official Blog, 12 Aug 2026: qualified watch hours and Shorts views](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-monetization-qualified-watch-hours-shorts-views/)
3. [YouTube Official Blog, 10 Aug 2026: YPP updates for 2027](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)

**Finding:** As of 18 Nov 2026, a channel can apply with 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified public long-form watch hours in the last 12 months, or 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. Hitting the numbers is not automatic entry; YouTube still reviews the channel against monetization policies (typically about a month). Shorts watch hours do not count toward the 4,000-hour path. The 10 million path does count Shorts, but only public Shorts viewed in the Shorts Feed as engaged views (the viewer stayed past the initial seconds). Loops, private or unlisted or deleted Shorts, ads, long-form views, and image posts in the Shorts Feed do not count. Other requirements include no active Community Guidelines strikes, 2-Step Verification, advanced feature access, living in a YPP country, and an AdSense account. Starting 1 Feb 2027, new YPP applicants will need 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. That change does not apply to channels already in YPP. Terms must be accepted in Studio by 31 Jan 2027. We already have about 1,010 subscribers, so the subscriber bar is met, but 12.7 hours is far from 4,000, and farther from 8,000 if we are still applying after 1 Feb 2027.

**Action for us:** Treat YPP as a long-term target, not a 16 Dec 2026 deadline. The realistic path is still public long-form hours, not Shorts hours. If we are not in YPP before 1 Feb 2027, the hour bar doubles, so the Monday scorecard should track hours gained, not “days until YPP.” Shorts still matter for discovery and for the separate 10 million / 20 million view path, but only engaged Shorts Feed views count.

## Channel changes this research supports

1. **Related video + a spoken and visual CTA on every Short, and start the long-form on the promised hook.** YouTube’s July 2026 guide says the link, a last-5-second CTA, and an immediate payoff in the first 5–10 seconds of the long-form are the conversion method; the link alone is not enough. Source: [YouTube Official Blog, 14 Jul 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/).

2. **Aim Wednesday long-forms at about 8–12 minutes of real content, and let Saturday question episodes grow past 4 minutes when the topic can hold it.** Videos under 8 minutes lagged a channel’s usual views in an October 2026 80-channel study, and only public long-form hours count toward YPP. Sources: [SociaVault, 8 Nov 2026](https://sociavault.com/blog/how-long-should-a-youtube-video-be); [YPP eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en).

3. **Write searchable long-form titles that lead with the bug and the question.** YouTube tells creators to use accurate, succinct, searchable titles with the important words first, and our impression counts show Browse/Suggested are not yet doing the heavy lifting. Source: [Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en).

4. **Put end screens and a House Guests playlist on every long-form, and do not mark those videos Made for Kids.** YouTube recommends playlists and end screens to help people watch more, tracks both as traffic sources, and hides end screens on Made for Kids videos. Sources: [Recommendation System](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387?hl=en); [End screens](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789?hl=en).

5. **Make the three Test & Compare thumbnails clearly different, and expect Inconclusive if impressions stay near 1,400.** YouTube picks the winner by watch time, not CTR, and says too few impressions or too-similar options often yield no winner. Source: [A/B test titles & thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en).
