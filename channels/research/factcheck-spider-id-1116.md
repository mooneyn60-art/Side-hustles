### 4. Description item: necrotizing-fasciitis "20–80%" line

**Source found; accurate as a quote of UC IPM if the official conditions stay; do not tighten it, and do not treat 20–80% as the current typical death rate.** Correctly kept out of speech.

The line is on the same UC IPM recluse page as the 10% and 29% figures (HS57), not on CDC and not in a named study on that page (the 29% MRSA figure is the one UC IPM attributes to "one national study involving 11 university-based medical facilities"). Live quote, confirmed this assignment:

> "Group A Streptococcus infection, also known as necrotizing fasciitis, is sometimes misdiagnosed as a brown recluse bite, and has a fatality rate that can vary from 20 to 80% depending on how quickly it is correctly diagnosed."

URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/brown-recluse-and-other-recluse-spiders/

**Scope.** This is a fatality range for that misdiagnosed bacterial infection, not for brown recluse bites and not for "spider bites." "Sometimes misdiagnosed," "can vary from 20 to 80%," and "depending on how quickly it is correctly diagnosed" all have to travel with the number.

**Accuracy of "also known as."** The official sentence is looser than the medicine. Group A Streptococcus infection is not another name for necrotizing fasciitis. GAS causes many illnesses; necrotizing fasciitis is one severe syndrome, and other bacteria can cause it too. CDC's clinical-guidance page on type II (streptococcal) necrotizing fasciitis says: "Multiple species of bacteria can cause necrotizing fasciitis. This page focuses on type II necrotizing fasciitis, which is also known as Hemolytic streptococcal gangrene." URL: https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/necrotizing-fasciitis.html

**The 20–80% range vs current CDC figures.** The same CDC page says: "In the most recent 5 years, the mortality rate of streptococcal necrotizing fasciitis has been around 15 to 20%." So UC IPM's 20–80% is a wide historical range tied to delay in correct diagnosis, not the current typical rate. If the description is edited, do not bare it down to "necrotizing fasciitis has a 20–80% fatality rate."

**Recommended wording if the description is rewritten** (not required if the description continues to quote UC IPM in full):

> Group A Streptococcus infection, which can cause necrotizing fasciitis, is sometimes misdiagnosed as a brown recluse bite. UC IPM says that infection has a fatality rate that can vary from 20 to 80% depending on how quickly it is correctly diagnosed.

Keep this in the description, not in speech. The 29% MRSA study can stay in speech (s6.l11), as now.
