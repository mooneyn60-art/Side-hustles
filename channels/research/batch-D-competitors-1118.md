### 33. Animated education channels (e.g. Kurzgesagt-style, small creators): what transfers to us

What transfers is the narrative formula, not the production scale. A 2026 breakdown of the Kurzgesagt method says the studio spends roughly 1,200 hours per video with a team of about 70, and tells solo or small teams to copy structure and research habits instead: one question per video, asked in the first seconds and answered by the end; one idea per scene; scale comparisons; one consistent look and narrator; a public sources document; and a hopeful or still-unknown ending. [Viducer — How to Make Videos Like Kurzgesagt](https://viducer.ai/blog/how-to-make-videos-like-kurzgesagt)

A separate explainer-formula write-up adds that the opening hook should put personal stakes on the viewer (“what would happen to you”), not a definition, and that each concept gets its own visual beat. [EditorDuel — Kurzgesagt’s Explainer Formula](https://blog.editorduel.com/blog/kurzgesagt-explainer-formula-munich-studio-23m-subscriber-education-brand)

For budget, AIR Media-Tech describes “strategic static” as the affordable move: still images with light movement, camera pans over artwork, and small loops (blinks, head turns), plus reusable assets, rather than fully animating every shot. [AIR Media-Tech — Affordable animation workflows](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/affordable-animation-workflows-for-creators-without-pixar-budgets)

A smaller animated-explainer case, Ink Explainer (77.8K subscribers), reports that an evergreen curiosity question, “What Did Ancient Humans Actually Do All Day?”, reached 3.6M views after they locked one repeatable format and schedule. [YT Wealth Media — Ink Explainer](https://ytwealthmedia.com/case-studies/ink-explainer/)

### 35. Ranking/tier-list videos: why they work and what the top ones do

YouTube’s own culture blog says videos with “tier list” in the title drew more than 1 billion views in the first six months of 2025. It attributes that to a color-coded S–D ranking that is easy to scan, works as both a long-form deep dive and a Short, and invites debate. [YouTube Blog — The tier list phenomenon](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/tier-list/)

In the animal niche, TierZoo is the clear model: animals are treated as video-game “builds,” scored on visible stats (intelligence, power, defense, mobility, health, stealth), and placed on an S–F list. A review notes that this makes zoology feel like learning a strategy, not reading a textbook. [Plugged In — TierZoo](https://www.pluggedin.com/youtube-reviews/tierzoo/); [Wikipedia — TierZoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TierZoo)

They rank for search-intent ID, “signs you have X,” DIY-versus-pro, and local service queries, not for myth explainers.

### 38. Kids vs. adult audience signals, and the made-for-kids risk with a cartoon style

Animation alone does not make a video “made for kids,” but cartoon characters are one of the official factors, and a wrong or overridden setting would break the Shorts-to-long-form funnel.

YouTube’s help page, citing FTC COPPA guidance, says a video is child-directed if children are the primary audience, or if they are one intended audience (“mixed audience”). Factors include subject matter, intended or actual audience, child actors, “characters, celebrities, or toys that appeal to children, including animated characters or cartoon figures,” child-directed language, child-appealing activities (songs, games, early education), advertising to children, and other audience evidence. [YouTube Help — Determining if your content is “made for kids”](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076)

The official FAQ says a video is not automatically made for kids just because children may incidentally see it, or because it is “family friendly.” One listed example of general-audience content, absent other signals of intent to target kids, is “an animated program that appeals to everyone.” A 13+ disclaimer is not, by itself, enough for the FTC to treat the video as not made for kids. YouTube also says not to rely on its systems to set the audience; it can override a setting, after which the creator can appeal only once. [YouTube Help — FAQ about “made for kids”](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541)

A 2026 explainer summarizing those policies notes the operational cost of marking a video made for kids “to be safe”: comments, cards, end screens, and personalized ads are lost. [FYProof — YouTube Made for Kids Setting and COPPA, Explained](https://fyproof.com/guides/youtube-made-for-kids-setting)
