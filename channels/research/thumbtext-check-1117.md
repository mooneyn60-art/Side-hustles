## 2. Cellar spider — `KWOM2-mNmww`

Posted captions match the on-box `script-longform-dont-squish-cellar-spider.txt`.

### A. SPIDER EATER — **PASS**

The cellar spider is shown wrapping a much larger house spider. Spoken: "She has been recorded eating giant house spiders, the big ones." And: "She hunts other spiders, even big ones." The live thumbnail already says "EATS BIGGER SPIDERS"; this line does not go further than the video or the source.

> "These spiders are opportunist predators and take whatever prey comes their way - including other spiders."  
> "Daddy-longlegs spiders have even been recorded eating the much sturdier and considerably heavier large house spiders (Eratigena saeva and E. duellica), and may be responsible for the decline of these species in some places."  
> — British Arachnological Society, Factsheet 3, *Daddy-long-legs spider*, https://britishspiders.org.uk/sites/default/files/2020-08/Daddy_long-legs_spider_preview_rev18.pdf (opened this session)

The Hampshire shed count (63 of 102 prey remains were common house spiders) is in the same factsheet and in the video. "SPIDER EATER" is a short form of that, not a recluse-control claim.

### B. 5X HEAVIER — **FIX** → `5X, IN A LAB`

The thumbnail puts "5X HEAVIER" over a brown-recluse-like spider. The posted video does say the number, then immediately hedges it:

> "In a lab in Brazil, cellar spiders killed recluse spiders up to five times heavier than they were. That was a South American recluse, in a lab. And given a choice, they went for the insects instead."

It then adds that a Kansas house study found some predation, "But the same study said don't count on it. As pest control, it's limited. Carol is not a recluse cure."

The source is the same lab, and the same species condition:

> "The predation on L. intermedia was greater despite P. phalangioides be until five times lighter. In the presence of different prey P. phalangioides preferred the insects instead L. intermedia…"  
> — Fischer & Krechemer 2007, *Revista Brasileira de Zoologia* 24(2), abstract (English), https://www.scielo.br/j/rbzool/a/L5p6Nm4bTrmK9NhWQQ3rKQJ/abstract/?lang=en (opened this session)

*Loxosceles intermedia* is a South American recluse, not the U.S. brown recluse (*L. reclusa*). Bare "5X HEAVIER" on brown-recluse art drops both conditions the video is careful to keep. `RATIONALE.md` already flags this.

**Corrected text (thumbnail length):** `5X, IN A LAB`

If the art is redrawn so it is not read as a U.S. brown recluse, that helps, but the lab condition still has to stay in the type. Do not use "EATS RECLUSES" or "5X HEAVIER" alone.

### C. DON'T SWING — **PASS**

Meatball on a chair with a shoe, cellar spider on the ceiling. Spoken open: "And you're standing on a chair holding a shoe. Give me three questions before you swing." Close: "Verdict. Put the shoe down."

## 4. House cat — `viD-nbPdaxk`

Posted captions match the on-box `script-longform-born-as-house-cat.txt`.

### A. ONE BITE — **FIX** → `ONE BITE?`

Cat sniffing true lilies (white trumpet flowers). The posted video says:

> "The FDA says true lilies and day lilies can cause fatal kidney failure in cats. A bite of leaf or petal, a few pollen grains licked off fur, or water from the vase can do it. Fatal kidney failure, in under three days."

The FDA page, opened this session, is the same claim, with the same three routes and "can," not "will":

> "Lilies in the 'true lily' and 'daylily' families are very dangerous for cats. The entire lily plant is toxic: the stem, leaves, flowers, pollen, and even the water in a vase. Eating just a small amount of a leaf or flower petal, licking a few pollen grains off its fur while grooming, or drinking the water from the vase can cause your cat to develop fatal kidney failure in less than 3 days."  
> — FDA, *Lovely Lilies and Curious Cats: A Dangerous Combination*, https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/lovely-lilies-and-curious-cats-dangerous-combination (page also: "current as of 09/16/2021")

Two problems with unhedged `ONE BITE`:

1. It drops the FDA/video hedge **"can."** Read next to lilies, it sounds like one bite *does* kill, not that it *can* cause fatal kidney failure.
2. A bite is only one of three routes. Pollen on fur and vase water are enough. The art is a cat *sniffing* the bouquet, which is closer to the pollen route than to a bite.

**Corrected text:** `ONE BITE?`

The question mark is the thumbnail-length hedge, and it matches the other two cat variants. Keep the flowers looking like true lilies, not peace or calla lilies (FDA separates those). `EVEN POLLEN?` is a stronger, still accurate alternative if a rewrite is welcome; it does not imply that only a bite is dangerous.

Do not change this to `LILIES KILL` or `DEADLY`.

### B. LANDS ON FEET? — **PASS**

Cat in an open high window. The question mark is doing the work. Spoken: "So always lands on its feet needs an asterisk." Then the Croatian vet-school series, and: "And that only counts the cats that made it to the vet."

> "High-rise syndrome was diagnosed in 119 cats over a 4-year period. … 96.5% of the presented cats, survived after the fall. 46.2% of cats had fractured limbs…"  
> — Vnuk, Pirkić, Galinović, et al. 2004, *Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery*, "Feline high-rise syndrome: 119 cases (1998–2001)," https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10822212/ (opened this session)

The video already keeps the selection caveat (only cats that reached the clinic). The thumbnail asks the myth as a question. It does not say cats always land safely, or that they always die. **Keep the question mark.** `LANDS ON FEET` without it would be a CUT.

### C. NO MILK? — **PASS**

Cat glaring at a saucer. Spoken: "And that saucer of milk from every cartoon? The ASPCA says milk can give pets diarrhea."

> "Because pets do not possess significant amounts of lactase (the enzyme that breaks down lactose in milk), milk and other dairy-based products can cause diarrhea or other digestive upset."  
> — ASPCA, *People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets*, https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets (opened this session)

The question mark matches "can cause," and the line is about cats/pets, not a poison-level claim. **Keep the question mark.** Do not change this to `MILK KILLS` or `NEVER MILK`.
