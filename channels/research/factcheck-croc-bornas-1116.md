## Special checks

### 1. Bite line: one saltwater crocodile, in one study, 16,414 N

**Pass.** s3.l1: "In one study, one saltwater crocodile produced a bite force of sixteen thousand four hundred fourteen newtons, about three thousand six hundred eighty-nine pounds." Card: `ONE CROC, ONE STUDY: 16,414 N`. Chips: `ABOUT 3,689 LB` and `ONE ANIMAL, ONE STUDY`. s3.l4–l5 then say this is the top of a range from seven adults, with a representative value of 8,983 N. No psi, no "crocodiles bite at 3,689 pounds," no 17-foot animal, no 6.7 m model prediction.

### 2. Teaser swap: "One crocodile's bite hit 3,689 pounds." — conversion and hedge

**Conversion: pass as a quote of the paper; not a clean independent rounding.**

Erickson et al. 2012, PLoS ONE, states both figures together: "Our datum for one *Crocodylus porosus* individual, 16,414 N [3,689 lbs] represents the highest bite force measured in any animal." Using 3,689 lb in the long-form ("about three thousand six hundred eighty-nine pounds") is therefore a correct quotation, and "about" plus "bite force of … newtons, about … pounds" keeps it as pounds of force, not psi.

Computed independently with the exact SI factor (1 lbf = 0.45359237 kg × 9.80665 m/s² = 4.4482216152605 N):

- 16,414 N = **3,690.01 lbf**, which rounds to **3,690 lbf**
- 3,689 lbf = 16,409.49 N
- The paper's 3,689 lb is 1 lbf below the exact conversion (consistent with a rounded 4.45 N/lbf: 16,414 / 4.45 = 3,688.54 ≈ 3,689)

Do not "correct" the long-form to 3,690 and contradict the paper. Keep 3,689 as the authors' stated equivalent. The hyena pair is also slightly rounded (4,500 N = 1,011.64 lbf; the paper prints 1,011 lbs).

**Hedge on the proposed swap: fix if used.** The current teaser hook is "One saltwater crocodile hit 16,414 newtons," with the card `ONE SALTWATER CROCODILE, ONE STUDY: 16,414 N`. That is the safer hook. The proposed swap "One crocodile's bite hit 3,689 pounds." is too bare:

- "One crocodile" drops *saltwater*
- "one study" is not in the hook (it is on the current newton card; it must stay on any pounds card too)
- "pounds" without "of force" is the opening the pack flagged as a psi mix-up (M3)

Corrected hook if pounds are used: "One saltwater crocodile, in one study, hit 3,689 pounds of force." (12 words, long for a 6–8 word hook.) If the hook must stay short, keep the newton hook, or use "One saltwater crocodile hit 3,689 pounds of force" and keep `ONE SALTWATER CROCODILE, ONE STUDY` plus "pounds of force, not psi" on the card and in the description. Do not say "crocodiles bite at 3,689 pounds."
