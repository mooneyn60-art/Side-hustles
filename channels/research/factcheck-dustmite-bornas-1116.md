### 5. Chapter 8 (s9.l8–s9.l10): "The same holds for" vs "The same is true of"; endorsement and label

**The synonym itself is fair. The shortening can over-claim what tannic acid does. Recommended wording fix. No brand is spoken; the label line passes.**

Live UKY:

> "Although benzyl benzoate will kill dust mites, clinical trials are lacking that show much improvement in allergy symptoms. The same is true of products containing tannic acid (e.g., Allergy Control Solution), which are designed to denature dust mite allergens so that they no longer cause symptoms. Treatment of the premises with either of these chemicals should be considered only as a supplement to more important allergy-reducing measures, such as encasement of bedding and removal of dust-laden furnishings."

And later: "ALWAYS READ AND FOLLOW LABEL DIRECTIONS FOR SAFE USE OF ANY PESTICIDE!"

- **"The same holds for" as a swap for "The same is true of"** is fair English. That is not the problem.
- **The antecedent is.** UKY's "the same is true of" points at the immediately preceding claim: clinical trials are lacking that show much improvement in allergy symptoms. The relative clause then says tannic acid products "are designed to denature dust mite allergens," not that they "will kill dust mites" the way benzyl benzoate does. Spoken s9.l8, "The same holds for products containing tannic acid," comes right after a two-part sentence (will kill, but trials are lacking). A listener can hear "tannic acid also kills mites." The card `BENZYL BENZOATE WILL KILL MITES, BUT CLINICAL TRIALS ARE LACKING; SAME FOR TANNIC ACID` has the same ambiguity.

**Corrected s9.l8 (recommended):** "Although benzyl benzoate will kill dust mites, clinical trials are lacking that show much improvement in allergy symptoms. The same is true of products containing tannic acid, which are designed to denature dust mite allergens."
