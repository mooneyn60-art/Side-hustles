# Breakout tactics past a ~1K-views stall: verified, ranked for Meatball Myths

Prepared Mon 11/16/2026, ~8:45 PM ET, for Social Studio. Free sources only. I opened every link listed here on 11/16. Nothing was posted and no one was contacted.

**Main caveat:** I could not verify a drawn-cartoon pet or bug channel that broke a 1K stall in 2025–26 with dated public metrics, so none is cited. What did verify: one animal-facts creator interview, one strategist's case studies, one large cross-channel dataset, and YouTube's own product data. The rankings below weigh that evidence against our numbers (`drafts/playbook-1116/analytics.md`, `drafts/tracker-1116.md`). We already do the basics (hook by 0:07, chapters, search titles, no batching, one look, Related-video links, reply-to-comment Shorts), so those are left out.

## 1. Post fewer Shorts per month and put the freed effort into long-form *(highest impact for YPP)*
- **Who or what:** Metricool's *YouTube Study 2026*: 799,718 videos from 71,177 channels, comparing Feb 2025 with Feb 2026. Channels posting no Shorts averaged **4,364** views per long-form. Channels posting about **5 Shorts a month** averaged **8,789**, which is 46% above the 5,985 global average. Channels posting **35 or more a month** fell back to the no-Shorts level.
- **Link:** https://metricool.com/wp-content/uploads/youtube-study-2026-EN.pdf (2026; the halo section is on the "nice-to-have?" page)
- **Strength:** large dataset but **correlational**. Metricool itself says "Causation and correlation aren't the same thing here."
- **Fit for us:** we're far past the ceiling, with **86 Shorts in 28 days** (Studio). Only 8 of 86 reached 1,500 views. Subs run about 1.02 per 1,000 Shorts views (Windsor, n=66 Shorts). The audience is 99.3% new and 0% regular, and the YPP card shows 10 of 4,000 hours. Shorts hours don't count toward YPP.
- **Apply (after the 11/30 verdict; the current cadence is approved through 11/29):** a 4-week test at about 1 Short a day (roughly 30 a month, under the 35 line). Keep only 15–18 s house-bug and spider Shorts in the winning format. Put the freed time into **2 long-forms a week** in the "bugs you should never kill" family. Success means long-form views and hours per upload go up compared with the 13 long-forms from the last 28 days.

## 2. "Run it into the dirt": clone the one format that over-performs until it flops
- **Who:** Mamadou Ndiaye (Casual Geographic, animal facts, TikTok and YouTube). One video, "animals that are way bigger than you think," did well. "I did the typical TikTok thing where something worked for me, so I decided to just run it into the dirt… 'I'm just going to keep doing this until it flops.'" The account grew to millions, he was approaching 20M followers at the time of the interview, and he wrote the book *100 Animals That Can F\*cking End You*. The book is the same premise as WMS.
- **Links:** Tubefilter, 04/05/2024: https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/04/05/millionaires-mamadou-ndiaye/. Paddy Galloway backs it up with "Ride it until they stop you" (Colin & Samir, 04/09/2025): https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway
- **Strength:** **anecdote**, one creator, and the breakout was in 2020, not 2025–26. It's here because it's the closest animal-facts parallel to what we make.
- **Apply:** our only proven format on both surfaces is **"bugs and spiders in your house you should / shouldn't kill"**. It has the 3 Bugs long-form (5.44% CTR, 42.71% viewed) and its sequels at 4.63% and 4.37%, plus top Shorts #2–5 (16–20 s house bugs). Make it a numbered, named franchise and use it for most slots until a 28-day median drops. For WMS, use a mild-language "Animals That Could End Meatball" list series, with every claim sourced.

## 3. Borrow a tier or comparison format from outside the niche
- **Who:** Paddy Galloway's case study of astrophotographer **Ian Lauer**, who went "from 2–3K views per video to over 1M." He changed from "I photographed the Milky Way" to "Photographing the Milky Way in 10 minutes, 1 hour, and 24 hours." Same source: "be familiar, but unexpected," and "steal formats across niches."
- **Link:** https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway (04/09/2025)
- **Strength:** **anecdote** from a credible strategist. No dated view chart for Ian Lauer was checked.
- **Apply:** sourced, drawn, mild language. Examples: "Your House After 1 Day, 1 Month, 1 Year Without Cleaning: Who Moves In" (Noodle as narrator, the organ cast reacting); "Meatball vs a Spider at 1x, 10x, 100x Size" (a WMS escalation, with sourced scaling facts); "Bugs in a $10 Motel vs a $1,000 Hotel Room." Test it first as one long-form plus 2 Shorts from the same footage.

## 4. Repackage long-forms that already get impressions, using YouTube's A/B tool
- **Who:** Paddy Galloway's case study of **Tim Gabe**, who improved a thumbnail on an *old* video and "saw the video snowball into 40x more views per day." YouTube's Help page says Test & Compare runs up to 3 titles and/or thumbnails and picks the winner by **watch-time share**, not CTR. It needs advanced features, can't run on Shorts, and YouTube recommends "testing older videos first."
- **Links:** https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway (04/09/2025); https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714
- **Strength:** the outcome is **anecdote**. The tool mechanics are official.
- **Apply:** *Weird Animal Facts You Didn't Know* has **5,262 impressions at 1.82% CTR and 11% viewed**. That's the most unused reach on the channel. Test 3 "never kill" style titles and thumbnails on it. Do the same for *Animals Thought Extinct Found Alive* (3,438 impressions, 2.53%). Retitle the videos that break our title rules (*Everything You Know About Animals Is WRONG*, *12 Animal Myths You've Believed*, *Conspiracy Theories… TRUE*). This needs Moondog88's okay because it's a Studio change. It costs nothing and doesn't add any new uploads.

## 5. Dub the best long-forms into one more language (multi-language audio)
- **Who or what:** YouTube, 09/10/2025: "creators uploading Multi-language Audio tracks… saw over 25% of their watch time come from views in the video's non-primary language" (data as of July 2025). Jamie Oliver's channel: "amplified views by 3x." The Help page says it works on videos *and Shorts*, translated titles become searchable, it needs advanced features, and YouTube's advice is "focus… on one or two languages."
- **Links:** https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/multi-language-audio/; https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784
- **Strength:** **platform aggregate data**, with likely selection bias because the early pilot was mostly big creators.
- **Apply:** this is a good fit for a drawn cartoon because there's no lip-sync. **Check Studio subscriber and viewer country first** (it's still on the gap list). Bart-era research found 709 of 883 subs came from India. If India or another market is large, add a single second-language dub (for example Hindi) to the 3 Bugs long-form and its sequels. Treat it as a test of watch hours by audio language. If the dub needs paid voice work, that needs Moondog88's okay.
