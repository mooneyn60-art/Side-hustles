### 1. Opening pair: "Your bed: bed bugs can travel over 100 feet a night." into "But they tend to live within 8 feet of where people sleep."

**Pass**, with a wording note on "a night" versus "in a night."

Live CDC *About Bed Bugs* (this assignment, page date Apr. 26, 2024):

> "Bed bugs can travel over 100 feet in a night but tend to live within 8 feet of where people sleep."

The pack's must-travel conditions: "can"; "over 100 feet"; "in a night"; "tend to live"; "within 8 feet"; "of where people sleep" (not "usually," not "of the bed," and not a 100-foot line that drops the 8-foot half).

How the script splits it:

- **s0.l0** (0:00.0–0:03.4, 11 words): "Your bed: bed bugs can travel over 100 feet a night." "Can" and "over 100 feet" stay. "Your bed:" is the you-plus-home frame.
- **The full official CDC sentence is on the hook card from 0:00**, including `IN A NIGHT` and `TEND TO LIVE WITHIN 8 FEET OF WHERE PEOPLE SLEEP`. Chips: `CAN`; `OVER 100 FEET`; `IN A NIGHT`; `TEND TO LIVE`; `WITHIN 8 FEET OF WHERE PEOPLE SLEEP`. Tag: CDC.
- **s0.l1** (0:03.4–0:07.1): "But they tend to live within 8 feet of where people sleep." The 8-foot half is spoken by 0:03.4, well before 0:05. "Tend to" and "of where people sleep" stay. Hook plus this line read as the CDC sentence back to back, without saying 100 feet twice in six seconds.
- **s1.l1** then speaks the official sentence in full, with "in a night." s1.l2 adds, correctly, that the 8-foot half is not "they never go farther." s5.l1's recap and s10.l2's verdict sweet line also use official "in a night." The long-form description opens with the official sentence, "in a night" included.

**"A night" versus "in a night."** CDC's exact phrase is "in a night." Hook speech uses "a night" to stay at 11 words (adding "in" would make 12, over the teasers' 11-max cap). In ordinary use, "can travel over 100 feet a night" and "can travel over 100 feet in a night" are the same capability. The risk is a rate reading ("100 feet a night" as a nightly habit), which the pack lists as do-not-say ("They travel 100 feet every night"). That is not blocking here: "can" stays, the official "IN A NIGHT" is on the card from 0:00, and the 8-foot half is spoken immediately, which undercuts a habit reading. s10.l4's callback uses the same 11-word hook, with the full official card returning. Optional tighter hook, if a 12-word line is allowed: "Your bed: bed bugs can travel over 100 feet in a night."

The 100-feet teaser uses the same pair (hook, then line 2 as the 8-foot half, with the full official sentence on the under-card from 0:00) and gets the same verdict. Its title uses "a night"; the searched question and description use "in a night."

UKY is correctly not given a 100-foot or 8-foot figure. s1.l3–s1.l5 tag UKY as a different page: prefer to hide close, but if necessary will crawl longer distances; during the day they tend to hide close to where people sleep; they may disperse if infestations persist. ### 2. Chapter 5 (segment s6): dryer, 120 F / 90 min, thermostat warning, whole-house heat, cold

**Pass.** Each live heat and cold line checks out. The thermostat warning spoken in the script is EPA's, and that tag is the right one.

- **s6.l1**, EPA IPM (last updated March 30, 2026): "Put bedding and clothing in the dryer at high temperatures for 30 minutes to kill bed bugs. Just washing will generally not kill bed bugs." Live EPA, exact. "High temperatures," "30 minutes," bedding and clothing, and "generally not" all stay. No other pest's dryer time is on screen. This is not the Mayo mite 15-minutes-above-130 F cycle. Pass.
- **s6.l2**, EPA IPM: "Heat infested articles, and areas, to at least 120 degrees Fahrenheit for 90 minutes to ensure that eggs are killed. That is often done using a heat-generating device or a specially constructed heating unit." Live EPA: "Heat infested articles (e.g., furniture, luggage, other items that can't go in a clothes dryer) and/or areas (i.e., a room in a house or apartment, or a whole house) to at least 120 ºF (approx. 49 ºC) for 90 minutes to ensure that eggs are killed. … This is often done using a heat-generating device or in a specially constructed heating unit, some of which are portable." "At least 120," "90 minutes," "to ensure that eggs are killed," and "often" a special device all stay. About 49 C is on the chip. The 30-minute dryer card stays separate and is not averaged. Whole-house heat is in the official "areas" parenthetical; the script does not treat that as a home-thermostat job. Vis tags the unit professional / special device. The official "higher the temperature, the shorter the time" clause is description-only, fairly. Pass.
- **s6.l3**, EPA DIY (last updated August 10, 2026): "Do not try to kill bed bugs by increasing your indoor temperature with a thermostat, propane space heater, or fireplace. This does not work and is dangerous." Live EPA DIY, exact. Said clean, after the official heat recipes, not inside a joke. Card and vis cross out the thermostat, the propane space heater, and the fireplace. Pass.

  **UKY versus EPA on this warning.** Live UKY does have a thermostat line, but it is not the same sentence, and it does not name a space heater or a fireplace:

  > "Efforts to rid dwellings of bed bugs by raising or lowering the thermostat will be unsuccessful, although professionals are able to achieve lethal temperatures with supplemental heaters…"

  The spoken line is the EPA DIY wording ("does not work and is dangerous"; thermostat, propane space heater, or fireplace) and is correctly tagged EPA. UKY's "raising or lowering the thermostat will be unsuccessful" / "supplemental heaters" line is not spoken, which is right: do not hang EPA's "dangerous" list on UKY, and do not hang UKY's "lowering the thermostat" clause on EPA. Pass.

- **s6.l4**, EPA DIY, with a safety-page chip: "Professionals have access to more intensive and proven methods that can even treat whole houses with heat." Live EPA DIY, exact. Card: `WHOLE-HOUSE HEAT IS A PROFESSIONAL METHOD, NOT A THERMOSTAT JOB`. Vis also carries the EPA safety line "care must be taken to ensure that it is done properly" (live EPA safety, last updated September 8, 2026: "Professionals can also treat your entire residence, but care must be taken to ensure that it is done properly."). Whole-house heat is said only as a professional method. Pass.
- **s6.l5**, EPA IPM: "Cold treatments below 0 degrees Fahrenheit for at least 4 days can eliminate some infestations. Home freezers may not be cold enough to reliably kill bed bugs. Always use a thermometer." Live EPA IPM: "Cold treatments (below 0 ºF (-19 ºC) for at least 4 days) can eliminate some infestations. … Home freezers may not cold be enough to reliably kill bed bugs. Always use a thermometer to measure the temperature." "Below 0," "at least 4 days," "some infestations," "may not," and "always use a thermometer" all stay. The script correctly does not copy the live typo "may not cold be enough." The −19 C figure is official and is a fair omission (optional on a chip). The DIY page's sealed-bag, 0 F for three days figure stays off this card and out of speech, not blended (D2). Pass.

### 3. Starvation: about 1–4 months vs "several months"

**Pass.** The two figures are tagged separately and are not blended.

- **s8.l6**, UKY: "In temperature-controlled buildings, a more typical duration without feeding is about 1 to 4 months. It is usually impractical to leave homes unoccupied in hopes of starving an infestation." Live UKY, almost exact (official puts quotes around "starving"). "About," "in temperature-controlled buildings," and "usually impractical" stay. A "just leave the house" idea is crossed out.
- **s8.l7**, UKY: "When infested dwellings such as apartments are vacant, bed bugs often disperse to nearby units, or reduce their activity until the unit is reoccupied." Exact. This is why a vacant-unit starve plan fails.
- **s8.l8**, CDC, flagged as a different page: "A different official page says they can live several months without a blood meal. That is not the same figure as about 1 to 4 months, and the two are not treated as one number." Live CDC: "They can live several months without a blood meal." Two cards stay separate. Pass.

The same UKY paragraph also says survival "is longer at cooler temperatures — potentially up to a year or longer at 55°F or less," immediately before the 1-to-4-month sentence. That colder-building hedge is not spoken. That is not a required fix, because the spoken line already keeps "in temperature-controlled buildings." Do not blend the year-at-55 F clause with CDC's "several months." Optional to add the 55 F hedge to the description if it is not already there.
