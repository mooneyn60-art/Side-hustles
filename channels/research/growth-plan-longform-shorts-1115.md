## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **4,000 hours before Feb 1, 2027 is not reachable.** It needs about 52 long-form hours a day for 77 days; we earn about 0.38 a day (10.6 h in 28 days, Studio 11/11) [team][estimate]. From Feb 1 new applicants need **8,000 hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days, plus 1,000 subs** [official, opened today]. Plan for 8,000. The first realistic money milestone is the **fan-funding tier: 500 subs + 3,000 hours (or 3M Shorts views in 90 days), unchanged in 2027** [official]. We already have the subs.
2. **Only a long-form format that breaks out gets there.** Steady small wins don't: even if every long-form did as well as "3 Bugs" (our best), the channel would earn about 630 hours a year [estimate]. A rolling 365-day window can never pass your yearly rate.
3. **The best-odds long-form is "Why It Sucks to Be Born as a [creature]", and it works on bugs and spiders too, the topics our Shorts already win with.** Zoozle (35.6K subs): Bedbug 52K views, Earwig 90K; Bound: Cockroach 72.7K; The Animal Person (46.3K subs): Tarantula 708K, all 10 to 12 minutes (yt-dlp, checked today) [team-verified public counts]. Add a midweek bug born-as next to the December Saturday born-as videos.
4. **The Shorts ceiling is one test batch that never gets widened.** Since Aug 24, 2026, a "view" counts from the first frame [official], so a typical 1,007-view Short is roughly 1,000 feed showings, of which about 44% chose to watch (Studio 11/11) [team]. Above 70% viewed, the median flatlines at about 1,150. So retention isn't what blocks the next batch. What blocks it is the swipe in the first second, near-zero engagement (0.27 comments and 0.14 shares per 1,000 views) and off-topic slots.
5. **The old Bartolozzi subscribers mostly hurt the long-forms, not the Shorts.** On long-forms, subscriber traffic brings 46% of views but only 25% of watch minutes: 0.49 min per view against 1.31 from Related videos (Windsor, 10/1–11/10) [team]. YouTube says Shorts are judged per video, with separate Shorts and long-form recommendation systems and watch history [official]. Don't start a new channel. Grow long-form through search and suggested traffic, and measure the subscriber share in Studio.
6. **Stop tuning what doesn't move.** Posting hour, weekday, Shorts per day and title tricks showed nothing in about 166 comparisons [team, research 100]. YouTube says no posting cadence is required [official]. Topic, the first second and engagement are the levers.

## 3. YPP thresholds (checked today)

From **YouTube Help, "Changes to the YouTube Partner Program"** (opened 11/15) [official]:
- From **Feb 1, 2027**, new YPP applicants (ads + Premium) need **1,000 subs + 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days**. Channels already in YPP are not affected.
- **Fan funding, Creator Partnerships and Shopping are unchanged:** **500 subs + 3,000 qualified hours in the last year, or 3M qualified Shorts views in 90 days.** That gets memberships, Super Thanks and similar, and Shopping. It does not include ad revenue.
- Shorts revenue from Feb 1 also requires keeping 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days. That doesn't matter to us yet.
- Shorts-feed watch time doesn't count toward hours, and neither do private, unlisted or deleted videos (YPP overview, S10).
- **Not documented:** whether a channel that joins the fan-funding tier before Feb 1 later needs 4,000 or 8,000 hours to add ads. Ask YouTube Creator Support or check Studio's Earn tab. It's moot unless 3,000 hours come before Jan 31, which needs about 39 hours a day.

## 4. Hours math (with our real numbers) [estimate]

**Assumptions:** hours = long-forms per week × 52 × average views per long-form in its first year × minutes per view ÷ 60. The rolling 365-day window caps hours at the yearly rate, so a pace below the bar never reaches it. Dates assume the pace starts Mon 11/16. Back-catalog tails are left out, so the math is slightly conservative.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|

**What it means:**
- **The 2027 bar is the real bar.** Even the breakout scenario passes 4,000 only after Feb 1.
- **Fan funding (3,000) is the first milestone.** It needs the born-as median plus a little more.
- **8,000 by Nov 2027 needs about 2,300 average views per long-form at 2 minutes a view, 2 a week.** That's a mix of mostly 500–1,500 videos and one 10K+ breakout a month. Minutes per view matter as much as views: going from 1.2 to 2.5 minutes halves the views needed.
- **Shorts path:** 20M in 90 days is about 222K a day, against about 2,750 total channel views a day now (about 80x). The 3M fan-funding Shorts path is about 33K a day (about 12x). Neither is a plan; both are only possible with breakouts.

## 5. Research findings

### 5a. Why Shorts sit at about 1,000

| Question | What the evidence says | Label |
|---|---|---|
| How does a Short get its first viewers? | A small seed audience of people who may enjoy the video; "depending on how that goes, it may get a lot more traffic, or it may kind of taper off" (Todd Sherman, Shorts product lead). Ranking uses chose-to-view vs swiped, average view duration, average % viewed, likes and surveys. | official [S1][S5]; seed quote reported in OnlineMarketing.de [N3] |
| Does the channel's history or upload count matter? | "Every Short is given a chance to succeed no matter the channel or the number of videos." Each video is judged on how viewers respond when it's recommended. | official [N2] |
| Is a 1,000 "view" a real view? | Since Aug 24, 2026, views count from the first frame and the old view is now "engaged views", the YPP measure. Our 1,007 median is therefore about one feed test batch; about 44% engaged. | official [S3]; team |
| Why doesn't high retention push us past 1,150? | Above 70% viewed the median plateaus (1,148 to 1,158). The batch is won or lost on the first-second choice and the satisfaction signals. Studio's "Stayed to watch" / "Viewed vs swiped away" card is the number to raise, compared against our own median. No official benchmark exists. | team; official [S2]; unofficial "70%" rule of thumb [S4] |
| Do the old subscribers poison the Shorts test pool? | **Not shown, and unlikely to be the main cause.** YouTube runs separate Shorts and long-form recommendation systems, says Shorts discovery doesn't feed long-form history, and personalizes by viewer history and topic, not subscription. Our Shorts get 93.7% of views from the feed. The subscriber share of Shorts views is in Studio's Audience tab; we haven't pulled it. | official [N1][N2]; team (research 94) |
| Fresh channel? | YouTube: "Separate them out when your viewers have totally different interests." Rene Ritchie: test on the current channel first, because it gets more data faster. A fresh channel would also restart the 1,000-sub requirement, which we've already met. **Verdict: no new YouTube channel now.** Revisit only if Studio shows subscribers are over 30% of Shorts views and swipe more than non-subscribers (our rule, not YouTube's). | official [N2][N4] |
| TikTok-first? | Our TikTok is weaker than YouTube: median 265 plays, against about 1,000 on YouTube Shorts. But search-intent TikToks broke out (news posts at 17,600 and 9,340). Keep TikTok as a search-led minute-cut channel (Creator Rewards pays 1 min+), not as the YouTube strategy. | team (tiktok-stats.csv, research 91/97/117) |
| Cadence and posting time | YouTube: no cadence required, and frequency isn't rewarded. Our data: no effect from hour, weekday or Shorts per day once topic is controlled. Metricool's audience peak is 4 PM ET daily, which our existing 16:00 slot already covers. | official [S1][S5]; team (research 100); Metricool |
| Topic consistency | House bugs and spiders: median 1,146. Other animals: 474 (p 0.001). Survival Shorts: 1,124. True/false: 493. News: 184. Conspiracy: 27. | team |
| Length | 17 s or less: median 1,187 at 90% viewed. 35 s or more: 285. YouTube says use the length "you need to tell your story". | team; official [S5] |
| Repost or remix winners? | **Don't re-upload the same file.** Deleting and re-uploading an identical file is flagged as spam (forum guide, unofficial), and since Oct 1, 2026 YouTube has been cutting reach for content that isn't original. **Do make sequels.** A new hook, edit and story on the same creature is allowed; rival sequels repeat (Paint Explainer 2.3M → Part 2 736K). | official [N5]; unofficial [N6][N7]; team (KNOWLEDGE) |
| Shorts → long-form funnel | The YouTube Blog's 3 steps (7/14/2026): Related video link, an explicit CTA in the last 5 s, and reward the click in the first 5–10 s of the long-form. Our hand-off is at most 0.49%, and long-forms get 0 views from the Shorts feed. Cheap, so keep it, but don't plan hours on it. | official [S7]; team |
| Case studies of breaking 1K | All unofficial, n=1. One case: cutting the intro, putting the action in frame 1, adding motion every 2–3 s and captions with SFX moved stayed-to-watch from 30–40% to 69.4%, and views from about 12K to 21K. Another: viewed-vs-swiped of 86.5% against 68–77% on the channel's other Shorts separated a 158K Short. | unofficial [N8][N9] |

**Single biggest finding about the ceiling:** each Short gets one test batch of about 1,000 feed showings (views now count from the first frame), and about 56% of those viewers swipe away in the first second. Retention above 70% doesn't widen the batch; the median stays near 1,150. So the gate is the first-second choice to watch, plus engagement that's close to zero (0.27 comments and 0.14 shares per 1,000), in a channel that keeps spending slots on topics that score 27–493. Cadence and time of day aren't the problem.

Read Shorts at 48 hours and long-forms at 72 hours. The baseline is the Meatball era.

| Shorts under 500 | 30% | ≤ 10% | ≤ 10% | Studio |
| Stayed to watch | about 44% engaged (11/11) | +10 points over our own median | +15 points | Studio Shorts card |
| Comments per 1,000 views | 0.27 | ≥ 2 | ≥ 3 | Metricool YTVP11 / Studio |

**Decision rules:**
- If the 11/16–11/29 test doesn't lift the Shorts median above 1,200, pull the subscriber vs non-subscriber split before changing the topic again.
- If a born-as long-form gets 4%+ CTR and 30%+ viewed at 72 h, move the next born-as slot ahead of the other long-forms.
- If the first two bug born-as videos beat the polar bear and lion on minutes per view, January goes 2 bug born-as a week plus 1 big-animal born-as.
- The compilation ban lifts only when a weekly long-form passes 500 views (existing order).
