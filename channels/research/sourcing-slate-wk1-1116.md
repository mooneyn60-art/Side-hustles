## B. Crocodile Short verdict, and the Queensland attack figures

Script under review: `/workspace/drafts/shorts-push-1116/wms-wednesdays-ep1-4.md`, EP 1 close: "Verdict: in the water, probably not. Nothing's guaranteed." Card cites CB50. Originality QA proposed: "Verdict: grabbed in the water? Probably seriously hurt. Nothing's guaranteed." ### CB50, live. **SOURCED.**

https://australian.museum/learn/animals/reptiles/estuarine-crocodile/ — HTTP 200. Author Cecilie Beatson. **Updated 19/11/20.**

> "Most attacks have occurred on swimmers or on people canoeing or bending down at the water’s edge. This low profile seems to elicit a greater predatory response than from a person standing upright, even if in shallow water. A person seized in the water by a Estuarine Crocodile has little chance of escaping without serious injury, if at all. Resulting wounds are usually horrific and likely to become infected."

The grammar "a Estuarine Crocodile" is on the page. Keep "Most," "seems to," "little chance," and "if at all." This is a warning, not a how-to. Do not turn "standing upright" into advice to "just stand up."

### Ruling on the verdict line

**The current line overreaches, and the proposed line understates the source. Neither should ship as written.**

- "Verdict: in the water, probably not" is ambiguous (probably not survive? probably not escape?). CB50 is not a survival-probability. It is about the chance of *escaping without serious injury*, "if at all." Reading it as "probably won't survive" goes past the page. The prior Shorts-push fact-check had passed this line; this re-read revises that.
- "Probably seriously hurt" is weaker than the official predicate. "Little chance of escaping without serious injury" already means serious injury is the expected floor. **"If at all"** covers not escaping — including not surviving. The next sentence, "Resulting wounds are usually horrific and likely to become infected," is stronger than "seriously hurt." A verdict that stops at "probably seriously hurt" drops the "if at all" clause and understates the page.

**Sourced line (recommended):** "Verdict: seized in the water? Little chance of escaping without serious injury, if at all. Nothing's guaranteed."

Keep "seized" (the Museum's word) rather than "grabbed." Keep "if at all." Keep "Nothing's guaranteed." Put the full CB50 sentence, including "usually horrific," on the card. This is a warning, not a tactic, so it does not credit a move with saving Meatball.

### Queensland attack figures. **SOURCED** (two official pages; do not blend them, and do not use them as the in-water verdict).

**1. Queensland Government, "Staying safe around wildlife."** https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/staying-safe-around-wildlife — HTTP 200. **Last updated: 20 July 2026. Last reviewed: 1 July 2025.**

> "There have been over 50 crocodile attacks on humans since 1985, with about a third of these resulting in a fatality. Unfortunately, for many of these attacks, victims were carrying out high-risk behaviours such as wading in the water while fishing, or swimming at night in known crocodile habitat."

This is the live wording behind QA's "about a third of 50+ attacks since 1985 were fatal." Official phrasing is **"over 50"** and **"about a third of these resulting in a fatality,"** not "50+."

**2. Queensland Crocodile Management Plan,** prepared by Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service, Department of the Environment, Tourism, Science and Innovation. © State of Queensland, **December 2025.** https://www.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0023/654260/queensland-crocodile-management-plan.pdf — HTTP 200.

> "Between December 1985—October 2025, there were 35 non-fatal attacks and 15 fatal attacks by estuarine crocodiles on humans in Queensland."

That is 50 attacks in that window, 15 of them fatal (30%, which is "about a third"). The plan's count is exact and dated through October 2025; the wildlife page, updated later, says "over 50." **Do not merge "over 50" with "35 and 15."** If a number is used, tag the page and keep that page's wording.

**Do not hang these figures on the in-water verdict.** Both QLD counts are for crocodile attacks in Queensland since 1985, not only for a person "seized in the water." A one-third fatality share across all attacks is a different claim from CB50, and must not be used to justify "probably not" (survive) or to soften "if at all." If the figures appear at all, keep the high-risk-behaviour sentence with them, as a description of past attacks, not as a how-to.

The plan also says, immediately above the count: "It is important to note that removing crocodiles does not guarantee the safety of recreational users of waterways. No amount of crocodile removal can guarantee an area is crocodile free and make water based recreational activities completely safe." That supports "Nothing's guaranteed." It is not a substitute for CB50.

## Unsafe to imitate (summary)

- **Dogs jumping:** do not grab, hold paws, push away, or put a knee up. AKC: that can feel like a reward, damage trust, and in some cases cause injury.
- **Kneading / claws:** do not punish the cat (iCatCare). Do not present routine nail trimming, or a home trim, as the kneading fix; iCatCare says most cats don’t need trims and to contact the veterinary team first. Do not show cutting the quick or forceful restraint.
- **Crocodile verdict:** a warning, not a how-to. Do not advise standing up, wading, or swimming as a test. Do not turn past "high-risk behaviours" into a safe-zone list.
- **Chocolate:** the call is to a veterinarian or animal poison control, in the source's words, with the amount noted. A consultation fee may apply at ASPCA APCC. No on-screen dose math that mixes units across pages.
- **Anxiety / night behavior:** frequent anxious behavior, or new or unusual behavior in a senior cat, is a reason to see a veterinarian, not a home diagnosis and not a dominance exercise. Tufts: force and "domination" advice is called unscientific and dangerous on the same anxiety page.
