## B) What's strong

**Dog shop:** a clear niche (doodle owners), a memorable tagline, and slogan-led designs that already sound like gifts. The cartoon style on His Majesty and Santa Paws is clean and consistent. Matching human tee + dog bandana/tank is a real differentiator and is already built in Printify. **Printables:** the first six products are finished, not just planned. Large-print + no-backwards-words + advent "24 puzzles" is a sharp angle. Listing images clearly show what's inside. Titles, tags, and descriptions are already much more complete than the dog-shop templates. Pricing sits on the file's researched medians. Covers share a coherent cream / forest / cranberry look (Lora, Young Serif, Work Sans, Bricolage Grotesque).

## C) What's generic / weak, honestly

The dog shop is trying to be three shops at once: ready-made cartoons, custom photo art, and an Etsy storefront named PrintanPress. `ADS_READY.md` and the Shopify policies contradict each other on custom art. The logo tagline "THE DOODLE SHOP" is nearly unreadable (dark gray on black).

Listing titles are a stencil: "Goldendoodle [Product], [Design] Doodle [Product], Doodle Mom Dad Gift, Cute Cartoon Dog Lover [Tee/Cup]." "Doodle Mom Dad Gift" is awkward in every title, including Doodle Mom itself ("Doodle Mom Doodle Shirt"). Tags are copy-pasted across products, including wrong ones ("dj dog shirt" and "funny coffee mug" on non-shirt/non-mug items; "summer dog shirt" on a mug). Descriptions are one short paragraph, reused, with almost no care, sizing, or gift language. About a dozen Printify designs have no listing copy at all.

Mockups are a weak point. Tee/mug/ornament "concept images" are flat illustrated blanks, not on-body or on-tree photos. The custom-photo mockups (ugly sweater, "World's Okayest Human" mug, memorial ornament) look templated; the memorial photo is soft, and the Warhol-style pop-art grid is the most generic personalized-pet cliché on Etsy. Doodle Mom art is cuter in idea than in execution (messy markings, standard varsity arch). The AI "royal" portraits are glossy but drift off the real dog; the runbook already notes 640×800 upscales and about one in four retries.

The ad creative uses the same flat mockups. "From $21.99" fights the price list (mugs $17.99–$18, ornaments $16.99 or $21.99 depending on the file). 75 Printify products is a wide spray for a shop with no recorded sales in these files.

**Printables:** covers for bingo and word search are almost the same template with the title swapped. No shop name on the images. Thanksgiving products are 11 days from Thanksgiving (Nov 26, 2026) as of this audit; the Oct 15 research said they needed to be live in October. Christmas listings were supposed to be live by early November for indexing. Four of the planned 10, including the January planners, are still unbuilt. "Dementia activities" as a tag is a sensitive search term and should be used carefully, if at all.

2. **Split the catalog in the shop, not in the docs.** Collection A: ready-made cartoon merch (ships on the Dec 5 cutoff). Collection B: "Your dog, your art" — Rookie Card, tarot, license, memorial, ugly sweater — with a proof email. Do not advertise custom art until payments, a test order, and the proof step actually work.

3. **For Christmas (now through Dec 5), lead with a short list, not 75 SKUs:** Santa Paws ornament + tee, Snow Day, Doodle Mom/Dad, and the matching Santa / Mom / Dad human-tee + dog-bandana sets. Write the missing listing copy for those dog items before adding more designs.

4. **Replace flat "concept image" mockups.** First photo: the cartoon or the customer's dog on a real product (Printify photo or a photo of Mac's tee/mug/ornament in a real room). Then: lifestyle (tree, cocoa, dog in the bandana). Hide or demote the illustrated blank tees.

5. **Launch the personalized Rookie Card and "The Good Boy" tarot as the premium gift,** in two formats: printed (ornament, mug, or 8×10, priced in the existing $16.99–$21.99 ornament / $17.99–$18 mug / $59.99 wall-art range) and a last-minute digital PDF after the Dec 5 ship cutoff (suggested $8–$12; idea only, not verified).
